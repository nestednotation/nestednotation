## 1. Context

**Why:** Today a Nested Notation session has exactly **one shared playhead** (`BMSession.currentIndex`). Every connected device votes together and advances to the same frame at the same time ([bin/www:332](../bin/www#L332), [database.js:311](../database.js#L311)). **Session Lines** breaks that single-playhead assumption so that, within one session, the flow can **split into multiple parallel "lines"** through the score — each with its own playhead, voting, and holding — then coordinate and re-merge them. Goal: a diverse, simultaneous experience where different participants traverse different routes (and even different sub-scores) instead of everyone being forced down one path.

**Source of truth:** the FigJam "Nested Notation – Session lines" (exported PDF), plus the locked decisions below. The old `feat/session-lines` branch was **explicitly ignored** — the feature was rebuilt from scratch on `feat/session-lines-v2`.

**Out of scope:** "Solving the Sound Transition Problem" (FigJam Section 1) — confirmed not part of this work.

## 2. Glossary

- **Line (flow):** an independent path through the score with its own playhead/voting/holding/history. A session has ≥1 lines.
- **Split:** a frame that divides one line into N child lines.
- **Rejoin / merge:** N lines converging into one at a named frame.
- **Track group:** a named set of frames that wait for each other's lines to arrive **and finish their holding periods** (arrival barrier + hold sync — the group unlocks on the last occupant's hold release), then run time-synchronized voting windows (tallied independently).
- **Hold-until (barrier):** a frame that pauses its line until other named frames have been reached by their lines.
- **Sub-session:** a line temporarily diving into a separate sub-score, isolated, then returning to the main flow.
- **Device:** one connected client (player or rider), identified persistently (see Device identity).

| ---------------------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `session-split="N"`          | main frame                 | Frame divides its line into N child lines; frame must expose N hrefs. Choice window → personal choice → auto-assign stragglers. | `session-track-group="name"` | any frame                  | Frames sharing a name **wait for each other's lines to arrive** (arrival barrier — every populated line that can still reach a group frame must land on one; empty lines excluded) **and to finish their holding periods** (hold sync, with 2+ occupants the group unlocks only when the last occupant's hold releases, so the lines leave together; a lone occupant never self-parks), then begin/end voting windows together. May list multiple targets when several of the frame's links are merge frames — the line merges at whichever it navigates to. **Independent of `session-split`** (owner decision): the attributes may coexist on one frame with no count relationship — e.g. staged merges, 3 lines → 2 → 1.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| `session-sub-start="Score"`  | `<a>` link in a main frame | The marked link is a **dive**: a line whose advance chooses this link (vote winner, no-vote default, or instant tap) enters sub-score `Score` (at its `START`) instead of moving. The link's `href` is the **return landing** in the main flow, reached on sub exit. Arrival on the frame itself is ordinary — the frame plays/votes normally. Root-level placement is a legacy validator error; a split frame's links may not carry it (`split-sub-link`). A frame may mark several links (even to different subs) — the chosen link decides. ## 4. Locked design decisions

1. **Lines are first-class objects.** Each line has its own playhead, voting/holding state, and history. **Every device is bound to exactly one line** at a time.
2. **Split = personal choice with a bounded choice window** (confirmed). At a `session-split="N"` frame a choice window opens (= voting duration). Each device taps its path and joins that child line. **Non-choosers are auto-assigned at window end to BALANCE line sizes** — keeps lines populated and minimizes empty lines.
3. **`session-track-group` = an ARRIVAL barrier + synchronized timing + independent link tallies + a global STAY override.** **Arrival barrier:** grouped frames don't just vote together — they _wait for each other_. A line landing on a grouped frame **parks** (frame shown, taps ignored — the hold-until banner protocol is reused) until **every populated line that can still reach a group frame has arrived on one** and every arrived line has cleared its own (out-of-group) hold-until. Empty (0-device) lines neither occupy nor are waited for. **"Device" counts every connection — admin included (L3, owner decision): an admin is a player with extra session controls,** so a line occupied only by an admin/SM tab is populated and IS waited for, exactly like any idle player's line; the admin's own tools (tapping the line forward, force-release, or closing the tab so attrition dissolves the wait) are the remedy. _(Exception: score-**map** tabs are observation-only and never population — see §6 Device identity.)_ The wait dissolves **timeout-free via reachability** (a line that goes dormant or navigates off every path to the group stops being "incoming" — same philosophy as #11); the SM force-release valve covers group waits too (panel entry `group:<name>`). A **late arrival** (e.g. a line revived mid-round) is not parked once the group stopped waiting — it joins the running round truncated (decided: lowest-complexity option). SM jumps never park at the landing (authoritative override, like the landing-barrier carve-out). Once released, grouped frames begin/end voting together (first tap opens all windows). Each line tallies independently; for **links**, each line advances to its **own** local winner. **Exception — STAY is group-wide:** at window end take the single largest count anywhere in the group; if it is a _stay_, **all grouped lines stay**; otherwise each line proceeds to its own link winner. (Stay = group-wide brake. Glow marker follows this.) **Tiebreak:** reuse today's random tie-break with leader-stabilization ([bin/www:236](../bin/www#L236)).
4. **Sub-session entry is a chosen link; return is decoupled via that link's `href`.** `session-sub-start` sits on an `<a>` link of a main-flow frame (owner decision — revised from the original frame-level, dive-on-arrival markup): taking that link IS the dive, and the link's `href` is the return landing. The sub-score only marks its own exit (`session-sub-end`) and never references main-score frames. All rejoin/hold orchestration after a sub-session lives on the main-flow landing frame.
5. **Sub-score location:** `public/data/[main-score]/Subscores/[sub-score]/` with its own `Frames/ Sounds/ Documentation/`. Owned by the main score (not a shared library score). **Sub-frames fetched on demand**; main-score frames stay inlined as today.
6. **`session-hold-until` = ALL named frames must be reached** (and each named frame's own holding period ended) before this frame's hold releases. **Rendezvous semantics:** a target counts once **any** line has reached that frame anywhere in the session and that frame's own holding period there has ended — tracked in a persisted session-global `reachedTargets` registry, read against the trails (§7.4) and the clock (§7.5). The waiting line stays parked at its own frame; lines never need to converge on the barrier frame. **Reachability guard:** a missing target keeps the barrier parked only while at least one active, device-bearing line can still reach it. Dormant, retired, empty and parked lines do not hold a `session-hold-until` target open — with one carve-out: a line **standing on** the missing target does, parked or not, because it has already reached it and its own holding period (or its departure) will mark it `done` on a timer no barrier gates. Once no still-missing targets remain reachable under that rule, the barrier releases instead of deadlocking. **Hold sync:** the barrier is a holding period — it extends the frame's own hold and never cuts it short, so a parked line is released only after its hold at the barrier frame has ended (the same rule the track-group arrival barrier follows, #3). **A release frees the line and moves it not at all** — a `rejoin-at` on the barrier frame is an announcement, never an advance (§7.3). **And a mark only counts while the room can still account for it**: coverage asks the registry AND the trails, so a passage replayed after a rewind waits again (§7.4) — AND the clock, so a target being played RIGHT NOW is not counted until that frame's own authored period is over, whatever holding state the line is carrying (§7.5).
7. **`session-rejoin-at` = merge** converging lines into a single line (shared playhead, collective voting resumes) at the target frame. Normally paired with `hold-until` to act as a barrier. **And one line per node, whatever the markup (owner):** two lines standing on the same frame are one line — the attribute announces where lines are meant to meet, it is not what makes them merge (§7.6).
8. **No reciprocal attributes.** Server builds the relationship graph at session-create / score-update; a validation pass runs before a score is used.
9. **v1 sub-sessions are plain** (no nested lines), but the data model + runtime must not preclude nested lines later (high-likelihood future).
10. **Backward compatible.** A score with no `session-*` markup behaves exactly as today: one line = the whole room.
11. **Empty lines go dormant & are revivable.** A line that loses its last device through **attrition** (→ 0 devices) goes **dormant**, not destroyed: it immediately **releases any barrier/rejoin waiting on it** (the awaited frame can no longer be reached — this timeout-free release is the primary deadlock guard) and clears its timers/voting, but **retains its frame, history, `subStack`, and pending coordination** so a later joiner can revive it (see #13). Revival is independent — it brings the line back into circulation (landing per the #13 fast-forward rule; the retained frame is the fallback) and **never re-arms a dependency that was already released**. _Structural_ removals are different: a rejoin-**absorbed** line is hard-retired, never selected by normal assignment/revival, and dropped from `lines[]` at once so its number returns to the pool — its merge undo does not need the object, it **re-creates** the line from the snapshot the rejoin took (§8.4). A split no longer removes anything — its first branch **is** the parent line, carrying on (§6) — but a line that a merge absorbs while it is still an undoable split event's parent is retired and **kept**, so that undo can restore it. The sole exception to "never revived" is an explicit structural undo — the split undo (§8.3) or the merge undo (§8.4), which brings a whole absorbed line back with its devices.
12. **A rider is a spectator, and a spectator changes nothing (owner).** A rider — a session page opened with **no password** — is audience: it cannot tap (the client's `handleSelectLink` returns on `!window.staffCode`, and the server now refuses every message from a rider except `MSG_PING`/`MSG_NEED_DISPLAY`), so it must not shape the room in any other way either. Riders are therefore **never population**: excluded from vote tallies, track-group and hold-until waits, attrition, dormant-line revival, adoption parking, split membership/balancing and `smallestLineId`, and never written to the `deviceRegistry` — the same treatment the score-map tab gets (§6), and for the same reason. They stay visible where the operator counts the room (`riders` per line, `riderCount` for the session). Two corollaries the owner spelled out: **a rider is only ever assigned to an ACTIVE line** (`spectatorLandingLineId` — preferring lines that have players on them, spreading spectators across those by rider count; it can never revive a dormant line, which it could not wake anyway), and **it may only ever view an active line**, so a line that goes dormant under a rider re-homes it (`rehomeRidersOffLine`, §7.8). _Supersedes the original "rider-only lines are folded into the nearest votable line": with riders out of population there is no rider-only line to fold — the line simply empties and goes dormant like any other._ A lightweight SM **force-release** remains as a defensive valve.
13. **Late join & path revival.** A device joining (or an unknown `did` arriving) after the room has diverged is assigned via `smallestLineId` over **live votable ∪ dormant** lines (ties → earliest id). A dormant line has 0 devices so it is always the smallest ⇒ newcomers **revive dead paths first**, then balance across live lines — but only when the revival actually brings a PATH back. A revival whose landing is a frame an ACTIVE line already stands on doubles a line that exists instead of restoring one, so the newcomer balances across the active lines instead (`revivalAddsNoPath`). The owner saw the other way round after a finished score: three players joining a room sitting on `END` revived three husks onto it and the map drew three separate lines on the one node. It is deliberately asked of the LANDING, not the frozen frame, so the case it exists for is the only one it takes: a missing slot of a live track group is still a path, and a joiner still fills it rather than crowding the covered one. **Revival fast-forward (revised):** a revived line does **not** resume its frozen (stale) position — it lands **where the room is**. While the room is still spread across the **latest track group it reached** (tracked in a persisted `latestGroupArrival` record, updated on every main-flow grouped landing, cleared on SM-jump rewind), that group is the room's position, and the landing is its **least-occupied frame** so revivals fill missing track slots instead of doubling covered ones (ties → the recorded arrival frame, then group order). Once **every line has left that group** the record is stale and filling one of its slots would send the joiner backwards into a finished passage, so the landing becomes the room's own front instead: the **least-occupied of the frames the active lines currently stand on** (ties → frame order). Whether the group is still live is read from those same frames (`activeFrames`). While ANY active line is mid-**dive** they are not asked at all and the record is taken at its word: a dived line stands on no main-flow frame, so counting it as absent from the group would read a room that is merely inside its sub-scores as one that has finished with them, and freeze a revival in place instead of landing it on the group those lines are about to return to. _A room that has reached the end of the score is the case that exposed this: `latestGroupArrival` still named a mid-score frame long after everyone had finished, so a player joining a session sitting on `END` was dropped into the middle of the score instead of being shown the ending (owner)._ The operator's forced advance of a straggler onto a group (`advanceLineToGroup`) shares this landing rule but deliberately passes no `activeFrames`: it names the group itself, on a barrier waiting at that moment, so there is no stale record to detect and a straggler lands even when it would be the group's first line. Leaving the frozen frame follows normal departure semantics (it is marked "done" in the reached registry); the landing re-evaluates the group's arrival barrier as usual. Fallbacks to the retained-frame revival of old: no grouped landing recorded yet, the recorded frame no longer resolves/is no longer grouped, the line is already standing on the frame it would land on (a slot of a live group, or the room's front itself — it revives in place), or the group is stale and **no active line is left to join** (the last line to finish the score is exactly this: it revives where it stopped, which is the ending). **A room with no active line at all revives the line it left LAST** (`lastVacatedLineId`, over the `dormantAt` stamp attrition writes), and only falls back to the lowest-numbered dormant line when no line carries a stamp (a state file from before the field). Numbering is not a position: a split's first branch keeps the parent's number even when nobody takes it, so a room that finished on `END` as `L3` left an empty `L0` on a mid-score fork, and with everybody gone the next joiner woke that husk and was shown the middle of the score. Stamped only by attrition, so a line nobody was ever on (an empty split branch, an unclaimed merge-undo route) never outranks one the room actually played; once that joiner has revived the finishing line, everyone after them is placed by the active-line rules above. Never assigned to a retired start/parent line. Dormancy already released a line's barriers (#11), so revival is **opportunistic, never a hold** — nothing ever waits for a revive.
14. **No-vote continuation fallback.** When a non-split Session Lines voting window closes with **no valid votes** and no retained in-window vote tally, the line is treated like a line of non-choosers and auto-continues by taking a default outgoing link from its current frame. For Session Lines, a valid vote is either explicit `stay` or one of the current frame's graph-derived outgoing vote ids; stale, malformed, or unresolved link ids are ignored. The default is chosen from the score graph's ordered `frameLinks` for that frame: one valid outgoing link is taken directly; multiple valid outgoing links are tie-broken randomly; no valid outgoing link means no synthetic winner is created and the line remains where it is (terminal/dead-end authoring is handled by validation/operator recovery). Explicit `stay` votes are preserved: if `stay` wins, the line stays. Split windows keep their existing child-line balancing behavior. Track-group windows keep the global STAY brake; if global `stay` does **not** win, a grouped line with no local link winner uses this same default outgoing-link fallback.

## 5. Validation (pre-use)

Scores are validated offline, before use — `lib/session-lines/validate.js`, exposed as `bin/validate-session-lines.js` (`npm run validate-score`, exit code by errors). Running it is the score author's responsibility: the server does NOT validate at build/reload time (a runtime console-only pass existed and was removed as redundant), so an unvalidated broken score fails at runtime (e.g. a wedged barrier), not at load. Checks:

- **`link-unresolved`** (error): every ordinary `<a href>` in the main score and every loaded sub-score points to a frame that exists in that same score (case-insensitive, matching runtime lookup);
- split node's `N` matches its href count;
- every `hold-until`/`rejoin-at`/`sub-start` reference resolves to an existing frame/sub-score;
- **`rejoin-not-linked`** (error): every `rejoin-at` target is one of its frame's own links (the merge is the frame's next step — owner decision; no split×rejoin pairing rule exists, the attributes are independent);
- each `sub-start` score has a `START` and at least one `sub-end`; the marked `<a>` has an `href` resolving to a main frame (`sub-start-no-return` / `sub-start-return-unresolved`);
- **`sub-start-on-root`** (error): legacy root-level `session-sub-start` is refused — the runtime no longer dives on frame arrival, so the old markup would silently do nothing;
- **`split-sub-link`** (error): a `session-split` frame's links are child paths, never dives — land the child on an intermediate frame that carries the sub link;
- **`ambiguous-sub-link-target`** (error): per frame, no two `<a>` may share a target when at least one is a dive. The runtime routes advances by TARGET FRAME (`subLinkForAdvanceTarget`, bin/www) — a pre-Session-Lines design that never sees which `<a>` was tapped — so a shared target is indistinguishable: a plain link + a dive means EVERY advance to that target dives (a tap on the plain link included), and two dives on one target means `find` takes the first and the second sub is unreachable. Both misroute silently in the room. One error per ambiguous target; duplicate plain links (no dive involved) are fine and not flagged;
- **`sub-dead-end`** (error): every landable sub frame keeps a path to a `session-sub-end` (no stranded lines — decided in lieu of a runtime terminal-frame hardening);
- track-group consistency: **`track-group-voting-mismatch`** / **`track-group-holding-mismatch`** (errors) require every frame in a group to use the same `voting` and `holding` attribute values (including all omitting an override and using the session default);
- **`hold-until-in-track-group`** (error): a `hold-until` on a track-grouped frame must not target frames of the frame's own group (the group's arrival barrier already waits for those; out-of-group targets are valid and compose);
- no obviously unsatisfiable barriers (best-effort).

**Deliberate non-check — main-score terminal frames (owner):** a line finishing on a main-flow dead end (e.g. `END.svg`) still counts as "coming" for open barriers; END frames are normal authoring, so a warning would be noise. Recovery is operator force-release or a rewind.

Surface results to the admin console (proposed "Validate score" action) and the dev console, mirroring today's console-based markup checking. **Hook points:** `initState`/`buildSVGContent` ([database.js:240](../database.js#L240), [database.js:345](../database.js#L345)) and `reloadScore` ([database.js:279](../database.js#L279)), invoked from session create/update in [routes/sm.js](../routes/sm.js).

## 6. Data model

Refactor the single-playhead `BMSession` into **session-global state + an array of `BMLine` objects**.

**`BMLine`** (extract today's per-playhead fields from [database.js:174-206](../database.js#L174-L206)):

- `id` (line id, e.g. `L0`) — a **recycled number, not a serial**. A line is a route through the score, and the same route should not keep collecting new names, so a split's **first branch (score order) continues the parent line** and keeps its number; only the extra branches take one, always the lowest `L<n>` still free (`allocLineId`). A merge hands the absorbed line's number straight back (`pruneRetiredLines`). A room that splits and merges all evening therefore still reads L0/L1/L2, and the same fork reads the same way every time it is taken.
- `currentIndex`, `history`, `historyIndex`
- voting: `isVoting`, `currentBeginTimeStamp`, `currentEndTimeStamp`, `currentVotingDuration`, `votingTimer`, `votingTimeStamp`, `didSendStopVoting`, `currWinningId`, `previousWinningCount`
- holding/standby: `isHolding`, `isStandby`, `currentBeginHoldTimeStamp`, `currentEndHoldTimeStamp`, `currentHoldingDuration`, `holdingTimer`, `standbyTimer`, `nextFrameHoldingDur`
- coordination: `trackGroup` (current frame's group, if any), `isBarrierWaiting`/`isGroupWaiting` (parked at a hold-until barrier / a group arrival barrier), `pendingHoldUntil` (set of frames still awaited), `pendingRejoinAt` (reserved — persisted, never written since the rejoin clarification), `subStack` (stack of `{score, returnHref}` for sub-sessions; stack ⇒ nesting-ready), `savedHistories` (stack parallel to `subStack` — main `{history, historyIndex}` saved on dive, restored on exit), `visitedSubFrames` (qualified `score/frame` visits, so `hold-until="Sub/Frame"` refs are satisfiable), `splitAncestors[]` (ids of the split events this line descends from — powers structural split undo)
- `dormantAt` — when attrition last finalized this line dormant (null for a line nobody was ever on); persisted, and read only to pick which line a joiner revives in a room with no active line (#13)
- `status` (`'active'`/`'dormant'`; dormant lines stay in `lines[]` so they survive restart and remain revivable — retired lines are structural and never revived normally, and are dropped from `lines[]` entirely — freeing their number — as soon as nothing can still restore one, which today means: no undoable split event names it as its parent)
- `uid` — durable route identity, minted per line object and restored by both structural undos. Numbers are recycled (§6), so this is what a structural event records beside them when it says which lines it took apart (§8.4)
- membership is derived: the set of connections/devices whose `lineId === this.id`

**`BMSession`** keeps the global, score-level fields ([database.js:174-223](../database.js#L174-L223)): `id, ownerId, sessionName, adminPassword, playerPassword, folder, listFiles, listFilesInLowerCase, listMultiChooseImages, soundList, hasSounds, isHtml5, fadeDuration, defaultVolume, defaultAutoplay, enableAutoplayByDefault, votingDuration, holdDuration, votingSize, standbyDuration, synTimeInterval, preloadDuration, isPause, isSessionDeleted`. **Adds:** `lines[]`, `graph` (see below), `deviceRegistry` (persistent `deviceId → lineId`), `subFrameCache`, and the persisted coordination registries: `reachedTargets` + `reachedGeneration` + `reachedGranted` (rendezvous, #6 / §8; the last is the rewind-landing carve-out, §7.4), `latestGroupArrival` (revival fast-forward, #13), `splitEvents[]` (structural split undo, §8).

**Connection tagging** (extend [bin/www:515-520](../bin/www#L515-L520)): keep `sessionId, isAdmin, isStaff, currentVoteTo`; **add `lineId` and `deviceId`** (and `isMapView`, below).

**Device identity:** the server originally had no stable per-device id — a refresh created a fresh connection that just re-read the session's single `currentIndex` ([bin/www:585](../bin/www#L585)). With lines, a refreshed device must rejoin **its** line. The moment a tab reaches the session, the client's `ensureDeviceId()` assigns it a UUID (`crypto.randomUUID()`) and persists it in **`sessionStorage`** (`did`), with an in-memory fallback if storage is blocked; from then on the tab is associated with that UUID. The `did` rides on every message (incl. `MSG_PING`/`MSG_NEED_DISPLAY`); the server keeps `deviceRegistry[deviceId] = lineId` so reconnects restore membership. An unknown `did` is assigned per #13 (smallest live-or-dormant line). `sessionStorage` is per-tab — each tab of one browser is its own performer; `localStorage` would collapse them into one line — and survives refresh and mobile tab-eviction restore; a closed tab mints a new `did` and re-enters via #13. It also keeps the id off every HTTP request and is generated client-side only (never baked into the shared, apicache-cached `${id}.html`). _Gotcha:_ Chrome "duplicate tab" copies `sessionStorage`, so a duplicated tab shares the `did` until refreshed.

**Map connections are observers, not population (owner decision):** the score-map page marks its ws envelope `mapView` → `conn.isMapView`; such connections are excluded from player/rider counts, vote tallies, group/hold-until waits, attrition, dormant revival, adoption parking, split membership and `smallestLineId` balancing (`performerLineConnections` in [routing.js](../lib/session-lines/routing.js) — the only routing helper `bin/www` imports). They keep a line binding only so history/graph payloads can be addressed. A line holding only map tabs goes dormant like an empty one. (Admin _session-page_ connections DO count — L3.)

**Riders are the other spectator (owner).** The routing helpers now draw the line between two views of a line's connections, and the distinction is the one to keep straight: `performerLineConnections` (minus map tabs) is what the operator is **shown** — it keeps riders, so the panel and the map badge still read `1p+1r`; `populationLineConnections` (minus map tabs **and** riders) is what the room **decides** with — every tally, wait, attrition check, split partition and balancing call. `isSpectatorConn` is the one predicate behind both, and it treats only an explicit `isStaff === false` as a rider: `isStaff` is assigned at `MSG_PING`, before a connection can carry a `lineId` to be counted under, so an undefined field (a pre-ping socket, a test fixture) still counts as population exactly as before. A rider gets its line from `spectatorLandingLineId` rather than `smallestLineId`, leaves no `deviceRegistry` entry, starts no attrition grace window when it closes, and is re-homed when its line goes dormant (§7.8).

**A map tab leaves NO footprint at all (owner decision).** Strengthened from "not population" to "invisible", closing the four places a `/map` tab could still be seen: (a) `ensureLineAssignment` returns for a map view **before** the `deviceRegistry` write, so an observer never persists into saved state — where a split's offline-device sweep would have spent one child's balancing slot on it, displacing a genuinely offline performer; a refreshed map tab just re-binds via `smallestLineId`, since the map renders the room from the broadcast `lines[]` snapshot rather than its own line's messages; (b) `countVoteForLine` tallies performers, so a map connection's `currentVoteTo` cannot count even if the page were ever given a way to send one; (c) the admin `lines[]` payload dropped its raw `devices` field — the SM line panel and the map badge both read `players`/`riders`, so no surface shows a number that includes observers; (d) `handleConnectionClose` returns for a map view **before** the attrition grace timer (fixed) — every count already filtered observers out, but the close path did not, so a map tab could still _start_ a dormancy window. Reachable, and verified by A/B in a browser: a device that goes offline before a split still gets a child line from the offline-device sweep, leaving that line **active with zero performers and no timer**; the map binds exactly there (`smallestLineId` picks the emptiest), and closing or reloading it made the absent performer's line go dormant. Broadcasts (`sendToAllClients`/`sendToLine`) and the `currentVoteTo = -1` resets still reach map tabs: the first is what the page is for, the second is a no-op on a connection that never votes.

**Relationship graph** (built in `buildSVGContent` / on reload, [database.js:345](../database.js#L345)): while reading each frame, additionally parse the `<svg>` root for `session-*` attributes and record: split nodes (+N + their hrefs), track-group membership, hold-until targets, rejoin targets, sub links (`subLinks`: frame → `[{score, returnHref}]`, parsed from `<a>`-level `session-sub-start`) and sub-end (→ score) mappings, plus per-frame ordered link targets (`frameLinks`). Produce a normalized model the runtime consults (no reciprocal attributes needed). _As built:_ the graph is **not persisted** — it is re-derived on every build/boot (the `toJSON` allowlist drops it), and it is assigned for **every** score, vanilla included (the [score map](score-map.md) presents a vanilla score as a single-line session); orchestration still gates on `hasSessionLines`, which is set only when `session-*` markup exists.

## 7. Runtime lifecycle

All voting/holding functions in [bin/www](../bin/www) take a `line` within a `session` (formerly just a `session`). Messaging helpers have a per-line variant (`sendToLine`, filter on `conn.lineId`).

1. **Session start.** One line `L0` at a `START`/`PRE` frame ([database.js:294](../database.js#L294)). All devices join `L0`. Identical to today until a split occurs.
2. **Line-scoped voting.** `countVoteForLine`: tally `conn.currentVoteTo` only over connections with `conn.lineId === line.id` ([bin/www:213-252](../bin/www#L213-L252)). `startVoting/stopVoting/holding` operate on the line; `MSG_BEGIN_VOTING`, `MSG_UPDATE_VOTING`, `MSG_SHOW`, `MSG_BEGIN_HOLDING` are sent only to that line's connections. Session Lines only counts `stay` or link ids matching the line's current outgoing links; stale/malformed link ids are ignored. If a non-split line closes with no valid votes and no retained `_lastVoteCounts`, `countVoteForLine` synthesizes a `winningVoteId` from the current frame's ordered outgoing links (`graph.frameLinks[frameNameForLine(session,line)]`) so the line auto-continues instead of falling back to `currentIndex`; no valid outgoing link leaves it in place (#14).
3. **Split.** Line reaches a `session-split="N"` frame → open a choice window (voting-duration based). Devices tap → recorded as their chosen child path. At window end: the line **carries on down the first branch** (keeping its number and its object, so its runtime handles survive) and one new line is created per further branch; distribute choosers to the branch matching their tap, **auto-assign non-choosers** (balance/default, `planSplitPartition`) — including the `deviceRegistry` entries of devices offline at the time, which are balanced like any straggler rather than left sitting on branch 0. Nothing is retired: every branch's playhead and history still start **fresh** at its own frame, the pre-split trail living on in the split event. The split instance is persisted (`splitEvents[]`); children carry its id in `splitAncestors[]`. From the score map an admin may structurally undo it (§8). **A split frame has no winning vote** — the line divides, so no single link is "the" outcome; `MSG_UPDATE_VOTING` there drops the shared `winningVoteId` and is addressed per device with that device's own destination instead (§11).
4. **Track group.** _(a) Arrival barrier:_ a line landing on a frame in group G parks (`isGroupWaiting` via `maybeParkAtGroup`; the hold-until banner protocol `MSG_BARRIER_WAITING`/`RELEASED` is reused — zero client change) while any populated line that can still reach a G frame hasn't arrived, or any arrived line is still hold-until-blocked. The wait is re-checked on every landing, dormancy, hold-until release, SM jump and post-restart rehydration (`canReachFrames` BFS over `graph.frameLinks` + pure `groupArrivalState` in the orchestrator; `tryReleaseGroupWaits` in bin/www; empty lines excluded; sub lines project to their return landing) and releases all parked G lines together — no timers. First device joining an empty/dormant line on a G frame re-parks it (`ensureLineAssignment`); a line arriving after the group released joins the running round truncated; group-parked lines are excluded from a round's resolution sweep. _(b) Synchronized voting:_ when any unparked line whose current frame is in G starts voting, the server starts the voting window for **all** lines currently sitting on a frame in G (synchronized start/end; parked lines excluded). Tallies remain per-line. At close, a global-max `stay` still makes every grouped line stay; otherwise each line takes its own link winner, falling back to its own default outgoing link when it has no local link winner. _(c) Split composition (S7):_ a grouped `session-split` frame still **divides** its line when the group advances — the group close delegates it to the split path (choosers land on their tapped child, stragglers are balanced across the children), never a whole-line link/default advance; a global `stay` brakes it un-split like any other grouped line. The group check runs **before** the split check at window close, so the outcome does not depend on whose synchronized window closes first.
5. **Hold-until (barrier).** On arrival at a `hold-until="X,Y"` frame, the line enters holding with `pendingHoldUntil={X,Y}` and the barrier-waiting banner (`MSG_BARRIER_WAITING`). The server marks targets satisfied as lines reach X and Y (and finish those holds). When the set is empty, release the hold (`MSG_BARRIER_RELEASED`) and proceed. If a target remains missing but no active, device-bearing line can still reach it (main-flow reachability, or qualified sub-score reachability via `Subscore/Frame.svg` — `tryReleaseBarriers` / `canKeepHoldTargetOpen` / pure `holdUntilReachabilityState`), it no longer keeps the barrier open; the barrier releases once no still-missing targets are reachable. A parked line counts for the target it is **standing on** (it reached it; only the `done` upgrade is pending) but for no other. Releases are gated on the parked lines' own holds: while any of them is still holding — or still in the 1 s standby that starts the hold — the entry stays closed, and the holding timer, the standby timer and the SM hold toggle all re-enter `tryReleaseBarriers` as release edges. "Still holding" means the frame's authored period has not run out (`landingHoldElapsed`), not that a flag is set (§7.5). _(Rendezvous: "reached" lives in the persisted session-global `reachedTargets` registry — `arrived` on landing, `done` when that frame's own hold ends; barriers release on `done`. A release FREES the parked lines where they stand and moves none of them, whatever their frame's `rejoin-at` announces: every merge is a co-presence merge, made on arrival — §7.3.)_
6. **Rejoin (merge).** When a line lands on a frame another line is standing on — a `rejoin-at` target or any other frame (§7.6) — the two merge: pick/keep one surviving line (`planRecombine` over the LIVE participants — survivor = lowest active id, keeps history; a dormant participant never survives a live one), reassign the other lines' devices (`conn.lineId`, `deviceRegistry`) to it, **hard-retire** the absorbed lines (structural — not dormant/revivable) and drop them from `lines[]`, handing their numbers back. Collective voting resumes on the merged line. Merging is by **co-presence** — no waiting; a score that needs a guaranteed merge pairs the target with `session-hold-until`.
7. **Sub-session (decoupled).** A line's advance choosing a `session-sub-start="S"` link — vote winner, no-vote default, grouped link winner, or instant tap (`subLinkForAdvanceTarget` at each navigation call site; matching is by the link's target-frame index, which vote ids and default fallbacks carry) — dives instead of moving: push `{score:S, returnHref:<the link's href>}` onto the line's `subStack`, save the main `{history, historyIndex}` onto `savedHistories`, load sub-score `S` frames on demand, set the line to `S/START` (`MSG_SUB_ENTER`). Rewinds, revival fast-forwards and other repositions never dive (they are not navigations). The line runs isolated (own voting/holding; no cross refs); frames visited inside record qualified `score/frame` entries in `visitedSubFrames` (this is what satisfies qualified `hold-until` refs). On reaching a `session-sub-end="S"` frame: pop the stack, restore the saved main history (the return landing is appended on top), set the line to `returnHref` in the main flow (`MSG_SUB_EXIT`). Stacks support future nesting.
8. **Attrition & revival.** When a line's last device leaves (attrition → 0 devices) the line goes **dormant**: release any barrier/rejoin awaiting it (#11), clear its timers, but keep its `currentIndex`/history/`subStack`/pending coordination. **Attrition is debounced by a grace window** (`ATTRITION_GRACE_MS`, ~8 s): dormancy and its barrier releases only finalize if no device reclaims the line in time, so a page refresh or a mobile network blip (the ws reconnect backoff is 1–5 s) can't irreversibly dissolve coordination. `ensureLineAssignment` cancels the timer on any (re)assignment; it includes dormant lines as targets (count 0 ⇒ smallest ⇒ revived first, #13); assigning a device flips it back to **active**, landing per the #13 fast-forward rule (retained frame = fallback). Split-parent and rejoin-absorbed lines remain unavailable to this normal revival path (retired lines are excluded from registry lookups and assignment fallbacks; a split sweeps offline `deviceRegistry` entries off the parent); structural split undo restores its specific parent explicitly. **"Last device" means last of the POPULATION** (#12): riders and map tabs neither delay dormancy nor start its grace window, and when the line does go dormant `rehomeRidersOffLine` moves the riders watching it onto a live line — spoken in the vocabulary that line needs (`MSG_SUB_ENTER` when it is mid-dive, `MSG_SUB_EXIT` when the rider is leaving a sub behind, a plain `MSG_SHOW` otherwise, with the pause placeholder restored if the room is paused), because a bare main-flow `MSG_SHOW` into a sub-view page blanks the display (§16). Map tabs are deliberately left bound: they paint the whole room from the `lines[]` snapshot, so their binding is a pure address.

### 7.1 Every landing plays ITS OWN holding period

A landing's hold comes from the **graph**, never from the tap: `landingHoldSeconds` reads the authored `holding` of the frame the line is standing on, and `nextFrameHoldingDur` — what the device reported — is only the fallback for a frame the graph cannot answer for. The graph is built for every score, vanilla included, and carries a `holding` key for every frame (`null` meaning "take the session default", which is an answer, not a gap), so that fallback is effectively unreachable.

**Whether a landing is held at all is the same question, of the same frame** (`holdsAtLanding`, the one gate every path uses). It used to be a second question with a different answer: the duration came from the landing while the gate read `session.holdDuration`, the room's default — which is **0** unless an operator sets it. So a frame authored `holding="12"` was ignored outright on the ordinary voted or grouped advance while every other landing in the census below played it, and the same frame reached one way held while reached another it did not. Both gates are asked **after** the playhead moves, which is what makes them answerable at all; a dive is excluded from the ordinary path entirely, because the line has not moved yet and `enterSubSession` starts the sub `START`'s own hold.

The reason is that a tap reports the `holding` of the frame its link **names** ([voting.js](../public/javascripts/voting.js) `getFrameHoldingDur`), which is the landing only for an ordinary one-line move. Every other way a playhead lands puts it somewhere else — or there is no tap at all — and each one of those was, at some point, timed by the wrong frame or by nothing. The census, so the next addition can be checked against it rather than discovered in a session:

| Landing | Hold | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary advance (voted, grouped, instant tap) | yes | the landing, off the graph like every other row — a `stay` holds its own frame, which it never left |
| Dive into a sub (`enterSubSession`) | yes | the sub's `START`; the link named the *return landing* |
| Sub exit (`exitSubSession`) | yes | the return landing; the tap named the *sub-end marker* |
| Split children (`resolveSplit`) | yes | each child's own destination; one window, several frames, no tap speaks for them |
| Rejoin landing (`runRejoin`) | yes, when the survivor travels | the rejoin frame; a co-presence survivor is already there and keeps the hold it is playing |
| Revival fast-forward (`fastForwardRevivedLine`) | yes | the frame it is teleported onto; the line was dormant, so no tap exists |
| Operator eject (`ejectLineFromSub`) | yes | the return landing — an eject is ordinary travel |
| Operator advance (`teleportLineForAdvance`, `advanceLineIntoSub`) | **no**, deliberately | it exists to END a wait; a fresh hold would be the opposite of the command |
| Every rewind (`roomRewind`, `lineRewindWalk`, `rewindLineOutOfSub`, `jumpScoreForSession`) | **no**, deliberately | an operator rewind is authoritative and does not re-enter the score's timing |

The rule that makes the distinction decidable: **travel holds, commands do not.** A landing reached by the score's own routing — including one an operator reconnects the line to — plays the frame as written; a landing that exists to end or undo something does not.

This also matters beyond timing. A landing with no hold is registered `done` on arrival rather than `arrived` (`recordArrival` reads `isHolding`), so `holdPendingAtFrame` reads false and a `hold-until` at that frame whose targets are already met releases **in the same tick as the arrival** — freeing the line the moment its device was told to show the frame (and, while a release still auto-advanced, taking it straight off again — §7.3). The track-group arrival barrier loses the same way: it waits for "the last occupant's holding period", and an occupant that landed hold-free has none to wait for.

### 7.2 A wait releases only once the line that completed it has JOINED it

`tryReleaseBarriers` keeps a satisfied `hold-until` shut while any parked line is still holding — `parkedLines.some(holdPendingAtFrame)`, the rule above read across the whole barrier. **Parked** is the set `enterBarrier` fills, and `afterLineArrivedInner` used to run the release straight off `recordArrival`, one step *before* that: a line arriving at a barrier frame and completing that barrier's last target in the same breath was not in the set yet. So the wait opened for everyone already parked while the line that completed it was left behind to serve out its own holding period alone, told to wait for a target it was standing on. It cost more while a release still auto-advanced (§7.3): the parked lines were moved off and merged without it, so the rendezvous the score wrote was unmakeable.

The arrival is recorded into `becameDone` now and the release is deferred: to `enterBarrier` when the landing IS a barrier (it parks the line, so its own hold counts, and runs the check itself), and to a trailing `if (becameDone)` when it is not. A line still inside a sub keeps its own early release — v1 subs hold no barriers, so there is nothing there for it to join.

It surfaces most sharply coming out of a **sub-score**, because that is where the two roles land on one line: the wait names a frame inside the sub (`hold-until="Tetra1/End.svg"`) and the line that completes it is the one the sub returns to the barrier frame. Browser-verified on a fixture of exactly that shape — before, the parked line left the barrier 3.2 s in, the instant the diving line surfaced; after, it held for the arriving line's full `holding="12"` and both were freed together (they then walk to the rejoin frame and merge there — §7.3). Nothing about it is particular to sub-scores: any line whose arrival completes the wait it is arriving at loses the same way.

### 7.3 A rejoin announcement never moves a line

**`session-rejoin-at` announces a merge; it does not perform one (owner: "rejoin-at should be an announcement that that note will be merged with another note or line in the next step — it should not automatically merge anything").** A `hold-until` release used to read the frame's paired announcement: a SINGLE target auto-advanced the released lines there and merged them — the Barrier→DONE pattern — while multiple targets were left to the line's own route vote. That made a barrier frame with one announced target a place the room passed **through**: the performers were shown their landing, it played one holding period, and the server then took the step for them. Nobody tapped it.

It is also the last route by which the long-running **"ending a sub-score skips its return landing"** report (§9, and the two entries after it in Appendix A) was still true. Those fixes each bought the landing its holding period — the deadlock guard, the hold gate, the eject's own hold, then the landing-hold census of §7.1 — and every one of them left the advance at the end of that period in place. Reproduced from the owner's own saved state on `-test- Session lines`: `L0` came out of `Tetra1` onto `E` (`hold-until="F.svg,G.svg"` + `rejoin-at="H.svg"`), was parked and held for its 3 s, and was then merged onward to `H` while the other two lines were still inside `Tetra2` and `Cube1`.

So `releaseBarrier` frees the parked lines where they stand and moves none of them. One announced target now behaves exactly like several: the freed line keeps its frame, its next window votes its own route, and **every merge is a co-presence merge** (`afterLineArrivedInner` — 2+ active lines standing on a frame some frame announces as its rejoin target). The barrier entry stops carrying `rejoinAt`/`rejoinAll` altogether — the graph's `rejoinTargets` is the only reader left — and the operator's force-release is a pure release too: the valve that MOVES lines is the straggler advance, which says so and is confirmed.

What the room gained is the frame itself. A landing that waits is now a landing the performer leaves, and the score's own arithmetic still converges: the announcement is validated to be one of the frame's own links (§5), so the vote that leaves `E` can only go to `H`, where the lines meet and merge. What it costs is that a rejoin is no longer **guaranteed** by the barrier — a line whose performer never taps stays put, which is the same thing every other frame in the score already means. The operator's valves are the remedy, as everywhere else.

One aggravating condition made the advance visible as a bug rather than as a feature, and it turned out to be the second half of the same report: the reached registry is a one-way latch that only a ROOM rewind restarts (§8.1), so a passage replayed after a per-line rewind or a merge undo (§8.2/§8.4) found its barriers **pre-satisfied** — `F`/`G` were still `done` from a pass 42 minutes earlier, though both lines had since been rewound behind them. With the advance gone, the owner saw what was left of it plainly ("hold-until not working, it reached but not wait for other lines"): the line now stayed on `E`, but its wait opened the moment its own hold ended. §7.4 is that half.

### 7.4 A replayed barrier gates again — the registry is corroborated

**A reached mark counts only while the room can still account for it (owner: "hold-until not working, it reached but not wait for other lines").** Rendezvous coverage was one question — is this frame `done` in the session-global `reachedTargets` latch? — and only a ROOM rewind ever restarts that latch (§8.1). The per-line rewind, the dive rollback and the structural undos deliberately leave it alone, because the ROOM did not rewind (§8.2/§8.3/§8.4). But those undos exist precisely to REPLAY a passage ("this replays the passage, it does not unmake it", §8.4), and a replayed `hold-until` met its own targets already satisfied — so it released the moment the frame's own hold ended and waited for nobody. On the owner's live state `E` waited on `F`/`G`, both marked `done` 42 minutes earlier, while the two lines that had since been rewound behind them sat inside `Tetra2` and `Cube1` with those frames gone from their trails.

Coverage asks a second question now: **can any line still account for having been there?** `registryClaimants` (pure, in the orchestrator) answers it from records the runtime already keeps, and `coveredHoldTargets` in the runtime is the only way the barrier code reads the registry:

- **Every line that is not retired** contributes its own trail. A line mid-dive keeps its main trail in `savedHistories`, so that is read too — and its in-sub `history` deliberately is not, because those entries are bare sub frame names (`START.svg`) that would corroborate the MAIN frame of the same name. The qualified form of exactly those landings is in `visitedSubFrames`.
- **An `active` (or `expired`) split event** contributes its parent's history, and a merge event each participant's snapshot. A split truncates the trails it forks and a merge takes its absorbed lines' objects away, so these snapshots are the only record that the room walked those frames — which is the same reasoning `ancestorGroupOccurrence` uses to place a rewound line (§8.1). An `undone` event contributes nothing: undoing it hands those trails back to real lines, and its evidence would otherwise outlive the rewinds that follow.
- **`reachedGranted`** carries the room rewind's landing carve-out. `beginReachedGeneration` pre-satisfies the landing frame's own hold-until targets because the room already passed that barrier — a grant no trail can corroborate by construction, since the lines have just been rewound behind those very frames. Recorded, persisted and counted, or the carve-out would be cancelled in the tick it was made.

Two consequences are worth stating because they are not symmetrical. **A rewind un-reaches what it un-walks for free** — no rewind site was touched: the trails are what answer, and every rewind already truncates them, so the per-line rewind, the dive rollback, the structural undos and the splice a post-rewind advance makes all feed the same answer. And **sub frames stay reached**, because `visitedSubFrames` is never trimmed — which is the dive rollback's own rule, decided by the owner ("the sub frames the line reached stay reached, because it really did stand on them", §10), now falling out of the records instead of being special-cased.

The latch is unchanged and still says what it always said: this frame was reached and its own holding period there is over. Nothing is deleted from it, so the saved state still records exactly where the room has been; corroboration is a reading, not a rewrite — which is also why it heals a session that is already running.

Browser-verified (`run-session`, sandboxed second server, `-test- Session lines`): `L1` walked `C → Tetra2 → F` (latch `f.svg=done`), the operator rewound it back to `C` from the map, and `L0` then came out of `Tetra1` onto `E` — **parked, owing `F`+`G`**, still parked through the whole watch after its own hold ended, where before the change it owed nothing at all. It released only when `L1` walked back onto `F` and `L2` reached `G`, and then all three sat where they stood (§7.3).

### 7.5 …and a target is not counted while it is still being played

**The wait ends on the target frame's own authored holding period, not on the reaching line's holding state (owner: "hold-until should also wait for other lines to finished their intrinsic hold period to unlock. Based on the target intrinsic holding period instead of general holding state to prevent holding block between hold until notes").** The latch only ever moves forward — `markReached` never downgrades a `done` — so a frame played out in an earlier pass carries that `done` for the rest of the session. Corroboration (§7.4) is what lets a rewound line put such a mark back in play, and on its own it put it back **on the landing**: the barrier unlocked the instant the line arrived on the frame, one holding period before it had actually played the note. Reported straight after §7.4 shipped, and it is the same skipped target as ever, arriving from the registry instead of the clock.

So coverage asks the clock as well (`holdRefStates` in the runtime, read by `registryCoveredTargets`):

- **`holdingNow`** — an ACTIVE line is standing on the frame with the frame's own period still to run, so the ref is not counted, `done` or not. A dormant line cannot hold a target open: nobody is on it, so it is no more playing the frame than an empty line is coming to it (#11). Where two lines share the frame it is the **last** occupant's period that ends the wait, the same rule the track group's hold sync follows.
- **`playedOut`** — the frame has been reached and has nothing left to play, so an `arrived` mark counts too. A landing is `arrived` until an EVENT completes it (a timer firing, a departure), and some landings have no such event coming: the SM hold toggle sets `isHolding` room-wide with **no timer behind it**, a state file saved mid-hold restores exactly the same way (§16), an operator advance lands the line phase-free on purpose (§10). The mark would then stay `arrived` for ever and every barrier naming that frame would wait on an edge that cannot happen.

**Both halves are read off the frame, and that is what keeps two `hold-until` frames naming each other from blocking.** A parked line's hold is EXTENDED by its own barrier (#6), so its holding STATE does not end until the barrier releases — while the frame's authored period ends on its own clock. Ask the state and `E` waits for `F` to stop holding while `F` waits for `E`: the room stalls between the two notes, which is the block the owner names. Ask the frame and both are played out on time and the room releases together. `landingHoldElapsed` is the one predicate: not begun (the 1 s standby gap), still running (a live holding timer), or flagged with the period unspent — otherwise the period is over, whatever the flag says. `holdPendingAtFrame` (the parked line's own gate) and the track group's hold-sync opt go through it too, so all three gates now answer the same question the same way.

_Bounded consequences, deliberately._ A wedged flag whose period has NOT yet run out does hold a target open until it has, where a `done` mark used to be enough — bounded by the frame's authored value. And the release of such a wait lands on the next barrier edge rather than on a timer of its own (nothing schedules a re-check for a hold nothing is timing): a restart's deferred check, the operator's next toggle, any landing in the room. Adding a timer to poll the clock was rejected — the wedge it covers is itself a state nothing else in the room is timing.

Browser-verified on the same score with `F` authored `holding="12"`: `f.svg` stale-`done`, `L1` rewound to `C`, `L0` parked on `E` owing only `F` (`G` genuinely covered). `L1` stepped back onto `F` at t+2.2 s — re-corroborating the stale mark — and `E`'s barrier released at **t+14.3 s, 12.2 s later**, exactly F's authored period. Before the change it released on the landing. The three barriers of that mutual rendezvous (`E`/`F`/`G` naming each other) all opened in the same tick with no blocking between them, and the `H`/`I` track group still parked and released on its last occupant's hold.

### 7.6 One line per node

**A frame holds one line (owner: "at each node you can only have 1 line — either that line was merged from other lines, it is still only 1 line at that node. So there shouldn't be any case that 1 node has 2 lines").** A merge used to fire only on a frame some other frame names in `rejoin-at`, and only when both lines stood on it at the same moment. Anything else left two lines on one node for good. The owner's own session is the case that showed it: the split at `J` divided four players 2/2, the `M` half walked on to `END` before the `K` half reached `M`, so the announced merge at `M` never met, and both halves finished on `END` as `L0` and `L1` — where every late joiner was then shared out between the two, which read as the joiners creating a line.

The rule is now enforced wherever a playhead lands:

- **On arrival** (`afterLineArrivedInner`, first thing after a sub exit, before the barrier and the track group): every other non-retired line on the arriving line's node — active or dormant — merges with it (`coLocatedLines` → `runRejoin` in place), and the survivor then meets whatever the frame holds through the recursive arrival `runRejoin` already made. So two lines reaching a `hold-until` frame park there as ONE line, and a track-group frame is never occupied twice. A node is the frame plus the dive stack: two lines inside a sub-score share a node only when they dived the same way (same sub, same return landing at every level), since merging them otherwise would have to choose between two ways back out.
- **After everything that does not arrive** (`settleCoLocatedLines`): every rewind gesture, the operator's barrier valves, and a revival. Those are authoritative repositions, so the merge re-runs nothing (`arrive: false`): it shows the survivor its frame and un-parks it, the rule every rewind already follows.
- **Survivor:** the lowest-numbered LIVE participant (`applyRecombine`), so the performers keep the line they are playing and its running phase when a dormant husk is absorbed. A merge of dormant lines only stays dormant — an active line with nobody on it would never be released by attrition — and a merged line that is active but empty gets the attrition grace window a disconnect would.

Every one of these merges is an ordinary merge event, undoable from the map. **An undo cannot leave two lines on one frame either:** a passage whose lines both came into the merge from the same frame (the `END` case above — both halves passed `M`) lands them back on it, where they merge again. The confirm says so — `A frame holds only one line, so L0, L1 merge again on ⟨M⟩.` — rather than promising a room the undo cannot leave standing. What the settlement must **not** do is re-create the very event the gesture just removed: `handleSelectHistory` marks the lines a merge undo has pulled apart for the length of that one message (`session.__justSeparated`), and `settleCoLocatedLines` leaves a crowd made up entirely of them alone. Reaching that at all means the undo could not land a participant anywhere but the rejoin frame, so it says so in the log; the next ordinary landing settles the room by the usual rule.

**A merge is scoped to its own room.** Line numbers restart at `L0` in every session, so `L1` names a different line in every performance the server is running. `applyRecombine` matched connections by `lineId` alone and was handed the whole `wsServer.connections` list: room A merging `L1` into `L0` rebound room B's `L1` connection to `L0`, wrote its device id into room A's `deviceRegistry` and into the merge snapshot, and told room B nothing — one performance disrupting another without a single malformed message. The list is narrowed by `session.id` once, inside the orchestration boundary, so no call site can reintroduce it. Observers are narrowed too, in the other direction: a `/map` tab or a rider in the same room **follows** the survivor for display — it must not be left addressing a line that no longer exists — but never enters the registry or the merge snapshot, which are durable performer membership and would otherwise hand a route back to something that cannot play it.

## 8. History & rewind

Session-lines rooms have **five rewind operations, all map-only in v1** — the room-wide checkpoint rewind (§8.1), the targeted per-line rewind (§8.2), the structural split undo (§8.3), the structural merge undo (§8.4), and the roll-back out of a dive (§10), which rides the same message and is told apart by its payload shape like the rest. The session-page history dropdown is a read-only trail display here (`available:false` always; the implicit bound-line `{selectedIdx}` jump was retired and is refused server-side). Vanilla scores keep the single-playhead per-step `MSG_SELECT_HISTORY {selectedIdx}` jump — their one playhead IS the room. See [score-map.md](score-map.md) for the menu UX.

**History is an undo trail, not teleport** — only visited frames are rewindable (structural split undo is the explicit exception: it restores a persisted topology snapshot). A landing that doesn't move the line (e.g. a room rewind that keeps it in place) does not append a duplicate trail entry (`setCurrIdxTo` skips the push when the trail already ends with the landing frame); any landing also heals trailing duplicates left by older sessions. Rewinds carry a **caller invariant**: whoever sets `historyIndex` externally must land the playhead on `history[historyIndex]` itself — every rewind path derives its landing frame from that trail entry, and `setCurrIdxTo` warns if one ever stops doing so.

### 8.1 Room-wide checkpoint rewind — `MSG_SELECT_HISTORY {group}`

Track groups act as **synchronized checkpoints** (FigJam reading (i): when diverged positions make plain history impossible, the room-wide rewind offers synchronized track groups as jump targets — a rescue tool, not a disabled control). Click or tap a common checkpoint group frame on the map → the server recomputes checkpoints on receipt (never trusting the client's list) and moves **every non-retired line** to its own landing in that group (`roomRewind` in bin/www; pure `commonCheckpoints`/`roomRewindPlan` in the orchestrator).

Semantics:

- **Checkpoint** = a track group EVERY currently-populated line has PASSED — its own trail holds one of the group's frames, or, when a split truncated that trail, the route its forks recorded does (`groupOccurrenceForLine`, the same test that places it there; see the landing rule below). Groups passed by only some lines are NOT checkpoints. Populated means performers and admins; **riders never count** (decision #12), and lines with nobody on them are not consulted at all — they are still repositioned. Legality used to read trails alone while the landing read the forks, and the mismatch cost the operator checkpoints exactly when they reach for one: a line born after a group blocked every group the room had crossed before its fork, so the menu emptied out as the room split and merged, and which checkpoints survived depended on which lines happened to be carrying untruncated trails. All common checkpoints are offered, newest-first (not only the latest); with loops, only each line's latest occurrence per group counts (v1).
- **Landing rule per line:** its own most recent trail occurrence of a frame of the target group (history truncates via `setCurrIdxTo`). Failing that, **the route its forks recorded** (`ancestorGroupOccurrence`): a split truncates the trails it forks from (§6), so a line born at `H` starts its history at the branch frame it took and a group the room crossed before that fork is nowhere in its own trail — though the line was demonstrably there, inside its parent. Every split event stores the parent's history at the moment it forked and `splitAncestors` names those events, so the walk goes NEWEST fork first (each older ancestor is a step further back along one route) and the landing appends, like a fill, since there is no index in this line's own trail to rewind to. Only a line with neither lands on the group's **least-occupied frame** (same occupancy logic as revival, `revivalLandingFrame`) — a guess, and it was being asked to cover a case the records answer exactly: rewinding a room to the group its lines forked AFTER put every restored line on whichever frame the fill reached first, so the owner's two populated lines (four players) both landed on `G` while `H` — the frame one of those routes had come through — took the line with nobody on it. The passes run in that order (history, then ancestry, then fill) against one occupancy count, so a guess never takes the frame a recorded route is about to ask for.
- **Mid-sub lines** are force-exited from their dive first (saved main history restored), then the landing rule applies. **Dormant lines** are repositioned like any other but stay dormant.
- **Availability:** admin-only; requires ≥1 common checkpoint; deliberately NOT gated on any history-availability predicate — rescuing a diverged/wedged room is the point.

**Registry on rewind — generation counter (option (b)):** an SM rewind bumps `session.reachedGeneration` and restarts `reachedTargets`, so every barrier met **after** the rewind — including ones satisfied before it — gates like a first pass. Carve-outs and details:

- The barrier **at** the rewind landing is already unlocked (the room passed it): the rewind pre-satisfies the landing frames' own hold-until targets (for the room rewind, the union across ALL landing frames, in one generation restart).
- Rewinding a **parked** line un-parks it (clears `isBarrierWaiting`/`isGroupWaiting`/`pendingHoldUntil`, drops it from the barrier entry) and sends `MSG_BARRIER_RELEASED` so the client's "waiting for other lines…" banner is dismissed.
- Landing on a frame with `session-sub-start` links needs no carve-out — dives are navigation-triggered, so any reposition leaves the line sitting un-dived; its next vote may choose the sub link (diving properly) or another link.
- Landing on a grouped frame **never parks** (authoritative override) and re-checks group waits the line was incoming to.
- `latestGroupArrival` restarts with the registry, then the rewind's own **grouped landing immediately re-arms it** (the rewind is the room's newest position of record — a checkpoint rewind always lands on a group, so revivals fast-forward there).
- _Self-healing, accepted:_ after the registry restarts, other lines' _current_ positions are not re-seeded into the fresh generation — a barrier whose target frame someone is already standing on sees it covered only when that line next moves (departure marks the frame done).
- The landing grants are recorded in `reachedGranted` as well as the fresh registry, because nothing else can corroborate them (§7.4): the room is declared to have met those targets, and the lines have just been rewound behind the frames in question.

**It walks the rejoins it passes back, and the confirm says so (owner).** The room rewind used to reposition every line **across** the rejoins in between and end their undoability instead of undoing them — so on `-test- Session lines 2`, where three lines crossed `LMO` and two of them merged at `Z` afterwards, rewinding the room to `LMO` arrived at a barrier the room had crossed with three lines and only **two were there**. That is the room-scale version of the walk `lineRewind` refuses to make ("why don't we undo merge automatically?" — owner): the checkpoint being returned to is a moment those lines were still separate, so landing on it with one of them swallowed is a room the score never produced. `mergesBehindRoomRewind` now names the rejoins made **since** the checkpoint and `roomRewind` takes them off first, newest-first, each an ordinary fully-validated `mergeRewind` (cascade, convergence, latecomer balancing and all), before the plan is **re-resolved** against the restored population and every line repositioned.

- **"Since" is measured in each survivor's own trail**, exactly as `mergesBehindLine` measures it for the per-line rewind: a rejoin whose survivor was already past its checkpoint landing when it ran happened after the moment being returned to and comes off; one the survivor reached the checkpoint _from_ is older history and is left alone.
- **A line placed by FILL contributes nothing.** Its trail holds no occurrence of the group at all, so it cannot say which side of the checkpoint its own rejoins fall on, and inventing an answer would take a passage off the room on no evidence. Such a line is dormant by construction (a checkpoint is only common when every _populated_ line has passed it), so its merges keep their ordinary map entry.
- **Legality stays the MENU population's question.** A restored line may have merged in before ever reaching the group, and re-asking "is this still a common checkpoint?" of the restored room would refuse half-way through a committed walk. `roomRewindPlan` already separates the two roles — `checkpointLines` decide, `lines` are placed — so the second resolve reuses the first's `checkpointLines` and places the lines that came back like any other — by their own trail, then by the route their forks recorded, then by fill. The lines an undo gives back mid-rewind are exactly the ones whose trails a split truncated, which is why the ancestry pass above matters most here.
- **Nothing is expired any more.** The expiry existed because a rewind across a rejoin left nothing describing the population; a rewind that undoes the rejoins it passes leaves every _remaining_ event describing a moment strictly older than the landing, which is ordinary history. `expireMergeEvents` is gone, and with it the hazard that one room rewind made every later one refuse. The only route to `expired` left is a state file the loader cannot corroborate (`migrateStructuralIdentities`, §8.4) — and `roomRewind` refuses **cleanly, before anything moves** if a rejoin it would have to take off is blocked by one, rather than repositioning across it in silence.
- **One gesture, one display drive.** The undos and the repositioning under them share a single `openDisplayHold`, so each device is addressed once at the end — the devices an undo hands back to a **restored** line included, which no `MSG_SHOW` of `roomRewind`'s own could ever have reached. That drive also subsumes the bespoke `MSG_SUB_EXIT` pass the room rewind used to make for lines it dragged out of a dive: `driveLineDisplays` compares the view each connection was in when the hold opened against the view its line ends in, which is the same test.

**And the confirm says what the walk puts back.** The confirm said only "Every line moves to its own frame in that group", so an earlier pass added `expiringMergeClause` to disclose the largest thing the gesture discarded — every merge undo in the session. There is nothing to discard now, but there IS a topology change the operator did not literally ask for, which is the same thing the per-line rewind's confirm already discloses: `restoredMergeClause` appends `N rejoins made since then are undone on the way, so L0, L1, L2 stand on their own frames again.` Counted per PASSAGE, like the menu (a convergence is one thing the operator watched happen), computed client-side by the same per-line question the server asks, and the receipt echoes it. Losing the future is still what a rewind IS ("we only offer the ability to go back in history, we don't offer the ability to go forward" — owner; the way forward again is a per-node valve, a straggler advance or a force-proceed, not a redo).

**And it stops offering to move the room where it already is.** Every checkpoint node carried a live `⏪⏪ rewind ROOM to ⟨CP⟩` button whether or not the room was standing on that group — so the gesture that repositions every line in the room was, at its own checkpoint, a button for a no-op. The per-line entries have marked their trail-end `current position` (disabled) since the beginning; the room entry now does the same, on the test the rewind itself would apply: every line standing on a frame of this group, a line inside a sub counting as standing outside it (its landing is a main-flow frame, so the real button stays on offer).

### 8.2 Targeted per-line rewind — `MSG_SELECT_HISTORY {lineId, selectedIdx, frame}`

An admin may rewind **one named line** to an earlier entry of its own ACTIVE trail (undo, not teleport) — on the main flow, or within its current sub dive ("a sub is a session of its own"). Admin-only; `frame` is a race guard (refused if the trail entry moved). Implementation (`lineRewind` in bin/www): resolves against the line's active frame list (sub-aware), cancels its timers, un-parks + banner-releases — and deliberately does **NOT** restart the rendezvous registry nor touch `latestGroupArrival` (the ROOM did not rewind) — it does not need to: the frames it takes off the trail stop being corroborated, so the barriers on the replayed passage gate again on their own (§7.4).

- **Track-group rule: passed ⇒ arrived.** A line whose trail already went through a group is never counted as "incoming" again, so a line rewound behind the group replays through it without waiting for the lines ahead (its own truncated trail makes it re-arrive for real). Implemented as the `hasPassed` excusal in `groupArrivalState`, wired to `mainTrailForLine` (on forward-only scores reachability already excluded the ahead lines; `hasPassed` makes the rule hold with loops).
- **Scope (revised, owner):** an in-sub line's MAIN trail is rewindable after all — every frame it has already played is a destination, reached by the **rollback** of §10 (`rewindLineOutOfSub`), which crosses the sub boundary on the way. Within a dive, the line's sub trail rewinds as before. The room rewind still force-exits subs. Map menu offers per-past-trail-occurrence entries on main frames (main-flow lines) and sub frames (lines currently in that sub).
- **Reaching behind a merge undoes it on the way (owner).** A merge is the one structural event a trail outlives — a split truncates its children's trails (§6), but the merge survivor keeps its history, so this menu legitimately offers entries from before the rejoin. Walking the merged line back there **whole** would stand devices that arrived by another route on a frame that route never touched: a room state the score never produced. So a rewind whose target sits at or behind a merge point (`orch.mergesBehindLine`, measured in the survivor's own trail via the snapshot's `historyIndex`, and matched on its `uid` — a fork re-mints the identity of the line that carries on, so an index into that older route's trail says nothing about this one; the map applies the same test, via `survivorLineUid`, before it warns — without it the confirm named merges the server then correctly left alone) **undoes those merges first**, newest-first — the only order §8.4 allows — each restored line landing on the node it came from. A merge that will not come off **refuses the whole rewind** (`merge-undo-refused`): the loop used to `break` and let the rewind run anyway, which produced exactly the impossible room this rule exists to prevent — the refusal carries `progress.moved`, so a walk that had already committed earlier steps says so. The rewind then continues on the clicked line alone; the undos truncate its trail, so the landing is re-resolved by frame NAME afterwards, and when the clicked frame went with the truncated tail the undos have already landed the line at or behind the target and nothing further moves. Siblings the undo brought back stay where it put them (their trails are their own; the requested frame need not appear in them at all). The map's confirm names every merge it is about to undo and which lines come back out, so the topology change is never a surprise. In-sub rewinds are excluded: inside a dive the active trail is the sub's, while a merge point indexes the main one.
- **Known interaction (verified, accepted):** the rewind leaves `latestGroupArrival` alone, but the line's NATURAL landings while replaying re-arm it (#13 — every main-flow grouped landing is the room's newest of record), so a dormant line revived during the replay window fast-forwards to the replayer's group, not the front of the room.

### 8.3 Structural split undo — `MSG_SELECT_HISTORY {splitEventId, frame}`

Rewinding an executed split collapses every current descendant **recursively** back into the original parent at that split node. That parent is normally the line that **carried on down the first branch** — it kept the parent's number, so it is one of the event's own descendants: the undo restores it in place from the event's snapshot and retires the siblings collapsing into it (whose numbers go back in the pool). An event recorded before splits continued the parent, or one whose continuing line was later absorbed by a merge, instead restores the retired parent object the split left behind — which is why such a line is kept out of the retired-line sweep. A `blockedByMerge` event keeps nothing of its own — but the block is not necessarily final: undoing the merge that set it (§8.4) lifts it, and that merge stays undoable for the rest of the session, so the block is only permanent once a room checkpoint rewind has expired it. The map says so: such an event is offered again whenever every merge holding it is `active` and is one of the steps the walk will take off — the split undo cascades through `undoStructuralChain` exactly as the merge undo does, so `splitRewindOptions` reads the same chain and its entry carries the same `cascade` the confirm names, and its count describes the topology the collapse will act on (the children the walk puts back) rather than the merged line standing on the subtree today. Unrelated lines keep their reached/checkpoint state; the reached registry and `latestGroupArrival` are untouched (a subtree rewind, not a room rewind) — the collapse restores the parent's own trail, so what the subtree un-walks stops being corroborated by itself (§7.4). Descendant timers/waits/sub dives and post-split topology are discarded; connections **and** offline `deviceRegistry` entries are rebound to the restored parent; parent history restored. Descendant-only later splits/merges are included; a merge crossing outside the target subtree **permanently blocks** that split's undo (populations can no longer be separated) — only the crossed split event is marked blocked. `splitEvents[]` is append-only in memory (`active` → `undone`), and ids never repeat (`nextSplitEventId` is persisted), so repeated visits to the same frame remain distinct across restart. An `undone` event is **not written to the state file** (`compactPersistedEvents`): nothing reads one after the fact — the menus skip it and corroboration ignores it (§7.4) — and a long piece would otherwise carry every discarded passage's snapshots forever. `frame` is the race guard. Events created before this feature was deployed have no lineage record and cannot be offered retroactively.

**A split undo is offered only when the WALK can run.** `splitRewindPlan` answers about one step and never sees the chain, while `splitRewindOptions` published `available` straight off it — so a split whose own collapse was fine but which had an **expired** event standing on it was offered, and the click bought `blocked-by-expired` from `undoStructuralChain`. That is precisely the shape the menu has spent so long learning not to have, and it was worst where it mattered most: after a room checkpoint rewind the fork's button was the only affordance left on the canvas, so the operator's last apparent route back was a dead one. `available` is now gated on `chain.available` as well, which is the rule the merge side has always had (`mergeRewindPlan` tests the chain before anything else). The refusal shown is the **plan's** where it has one and the chain's otherwise: `mixed-merge` says which merge mixed the subtree and that it can never come off, where `blocked-by-expired` only says something in the way expired. Two smaller things went with it. A collapse with **nothing left to join up** reads `(1 line, back to this frame)` rather than `(1 line → 1)`, which described a button that does nothing when what it does is put the line back on the fork and discard the branch it played. And the menu's catch-all refusal note now prints the operator's words from the same table the refusal **alert** reads, so the note and the alert it saves say the same thing.

### 8.4 Structural merge undo — `MSG_SELECT_HISTORY {mergeEventId, frame}`

Rewinding an executed **rejoin** separates the merged line again: every line the merge swallowed comes back **on the node it came into the merge from**, with its trail, dive and track group as the merge found them, and the survivor goes back to its own too — nobody keeps the merged line's route. That landing reads the same for both merge paths, which take the lines in different places (`preMergeLanding`): a **barrier release** merges lines still parked on their own frames (`E`/`F`/`G` rejoining at `H`), so each snapshot already names the frame it came from and nothing is stepped back; a **co-presence** merge fires when lines walk ONTO the rejoin frame (`V`/`W` → `Z`), so every snapshot names that frame itself and the undo steps each line back one entry of its own trail — the trail truncating as any rewind's does, past consecutive re-visits of the rejoin frame. A line mid-**dive** is stepped back the same way, INSIDE its sub: its trail is the sub's, its index indexes the sub's frame list, and its return context (`subStack`/`savedHistories`) is restored untouched — so the only thing that changes is which frame list the predecessor resolves against (`frameIndexResolver`). Returning null for every dived snapshot, as this used to, is the same answer as "it is already back home": two lines that merged on a frame inside a sub came back standing ON that frame, and the settlement merged them again on the spot, so the gesture reported a separation it had already undone. Frame names are unique within a score and not across them, so the merge event records the score it happened in (`sub`) — without it a rejoin on `End.svg` inside `Tetra1` was folded into a convergence with a main-flow rejoin of the same name, and the map's `⏪⏪` badge and menu landed on an unrelated main node while the sub node the operator watched it happen on offered nothing. One carve-out remains: a line with nothing behind it keeps its snapshot position — a line a split seeded on the rejoin frame itself, or one whose previous frame the score no longer has. Each device returns to the line it was on then (`conn.lineId` **and** the offline `deviceRegistry` entries, from the snapshot's per-line `deviceIds`); a device whose first sight of the room came **after** the merge is in no snapshot and stays with the survivor, since it has no pre-merge line to be put back on. A restored line nobody came back to lands **dormant**, not retired — #11 revives it for the next joiner and releases anything waiting on it meanwhile; "nobody" means no POPULATION, so a rider riding that route along cannot hold it active (decision #12 — counting one did, and the line then read active with zero players, so nothing released its waits and #13 never fast-forwarded it). A rider present at the merge does go back to the line it was watching: following the route it was actually following is the view it asked for, and it is outside the balancing either way. Like the split undo this is a **subtree** rewind: the reached registry and `latestGroupArrival` are untouched (the ROOM did not rewind), and the event going `undone` is what stops its snapshot corroborating the passage it recorded (§7.4), and the restored lines land **un-parked and phase-less** — an operator rewind is authoritative. Their next window is an ordinary one, so a route that leads back to the rejoin frame merges them again: this **replays the passage**, it does not unmake it. `frame` (the rejoin frame) is the race guard, and the map offers the undo on that node — where the operator watched it happen.

**A per-line rewind reaching back past a merge undoes it too** — see §8.2. The explicit entry stays the direct way to separate a merge without moving anyone further; the implicit one exists because the alternative was a rewind that silently produced an impossible room.

**A merge point stays reachable for the whole session (owner: "able to rewind back to multiple merge point later still possible, not losing the route path").** A rejoin used to stop being undoable the moment the merged line took its next structural step — it forked, and the passage was gone for the evening — so on a score shaped `FORK → MERGE1 → FORK2 → MERGE2` the only reachable merge point was ever the newest one. Nothing expires on a structural step now: `structuralRewindChain` walks the events the room built ON TOP of the one being reached for — later merges _and_ later splits, transitively over the same population — and the runtime undoes them **newest first** before the target, each step an ordinary fully-validated snapshot restore (`undoStructuralChain`). One click on `MERGE1` therefore takes off the `MERGE2` convergence and the `FORK2` split on the way, and the confirm names them before the operator commits. Three things make that safe rather than approximate. **Identity**: line NUMBERS are recycled, so every line carries a durable `uid` and every structural event records the participants' uids beside their numbers — the `L2` a later fork minted is not the `L2` an older merge swallowed, so an unrelated branch is never dragged into a cascade. **Ordering**: splits and merges have separate id counters, so both kinds now carry one session-wide `seq` — the only thing that says which of an `S` and an `M` happened first. **Numbers**: an absorbed line's object is no longer held for its undo (`reservedMergeLineIds` is gone) — its number goes straight back in the pool at the rejoin and the undo RE-CREATES the line from the snapshot, asking for the number it had and taking the lowest free one if a fork has since been handed it. The room's `L0/L1/L2` reading (§6) is therefore unchanged by keeping merges undoable for ever. A **room checkpoint rewind** used to be the one thing that still expired a merge; it now undoes the rejoins it rewinds past instead (§8.1) and expires nothing, so the only `expired` events left are the ones a state file arrives with (`migrateStructuralIdentities`, below). The RECORD survives expiry either way — nothing is compacted away, so the saved state still says exactly where every line walked — but the map stops DRAWING such a passage, under the same rule that hides an older passage's routes (below): a ghost is only for a passage the room can still be walked back through, and an `L1` whose number went back in the pool an hour ago is not the `L1` the room is playing. What the node carries instead is the note saying why this passage offers no undo, and the entry behind it carries nothing else: `mergeRewindOptions` ships an expired event as its frame and its reason, those being the only two fields anything reads (it used to ship the cascade, the line list and the trail index too, on every push, for an undo that can never run again). **Devices that joined after the merge** are no longer left on the survivor wholesale: they are **spread across the lines coming back** (owner), the same balancing a split gives the members it finds unassigned, live and offline alike — riders excepted, since a spectator is never population (decision #12). The confirm's count of them (`mergeLatecomerCount`, printed only when there is somebody to spread) counts offline registry entries too: counting live connections alone let it say nothing at all while a dozen registered-but-absent devices were about to be redistributed. **And the spread is the PASSAGE's, not the step's.** The count always reasoned over the whole convergence; the distribution did not — it ran inside `rewindMergeStructure`, once per event, and a convergence undoes in pairs, so the first step could only see two of the three lines coming back and spent the latecomers against a shorter list of destinations. Three latecomers over three lines came out **2/1/3** in the browser while the confirm promised them "spread evenly". `spreadMergeLatecomers` is now its own pass — the balancing, the offline sweep and the dormancy verdict together — and the runtime runs it once more over the whole passage when the walk is done, seeded from the union of every step's snapshot devices. It re-assigns every latecomer from scratch, so its answer is one canonical arrangement rather than "whatever is already even": asked twice it moves nobody the second time, which is what makes the repeat safe for a merge that was a single event. The displays moved with it — `mergeRewindOne` no longer drives them, `sendRestoredLineDisplays` does, once, after the balancing, so no device is told about a line the next step is about to move it off (and the survivor stops getting the same three messages once per event of the passage).

**And the lines the merge SWALLOWED are rewindable too (owner).** After a rejoin the only route left on the map was the survivor's, so `A1/A2` offered "rewind L0 here" while `B1/B2` and `C1/C2` — where the operator actually watched L1 and L2 walk — offered nothing at all: the one line you could reach for was the one that had absorbed everybody. An absorbed line is retired, but while its merge is still undoable its object (and its trail) is kept for exactly that undo, so the map now publishes it in a separate `absorbedLines[]` array — never `lines[]`, since a retired line must stay out of every count and panel — paints its route as a **ghost** (dashed grey, a halo on the node it would land on) and offers the same per-line rewind on it. `lineRewind` accepts a retired line by bringing it back first: it undoes the merge that took it, whole (§8.4's convergence rule), so every line that met there lands on the node it came from, and then walks the clicked line on alone. The room state is identical to rewinding the SURVIVOR past the same merge — this is that same act, reached from the line the operator remembers. Two details make it read right: the trail published is the one the undo will RESTORE (`preMergeLanding` already steps a co-presence snapshot back off the rejoin frame), so every entry is a real destination; and its LAST entry is offered, unlike a live line's, because a ghost is standing nowhere — that entry is "bring it back where it was", and the undo alone satisfies it. The race guard is checked before the undo, so a stale menu cannot buy a topology change. The swallowed line has no object at all — the snapshot IS the line until the undo re-creates it — so the entry carries its `mergeEventId` (`absorbedEventId` in the message) and the server finds the route through the event; after the undo the line is looked up by `uid`, because it may have come back on a different number. **And the survivor's own route into the rejoin is a ghost too (owner: "all lines should be equal … the map has no main line, it only lines").** The map used to paint the survivor's pre-merge trail as live green history while its co-arrivals' routes were ghosts — and which route got the green was decided by nothing but `planRecombine` electing the lowest-numbered line as survivor, so `L0` won every merge it took part in and read as a main line this model does not have. Behind a still-undoable rejoin the survivor's route is exactly as past as the others': nobody is standing on it, and the same one undo gives all of them back. The merge entry therefore carries `survivorTrail`/`survivorLanding`/`survivorSub` (`survivorGhostRoute` in bin/www, built by the same `absorbedRoute` helper off the OLDEST member of the convergence — the position the whole passage's undo restores the survivor to, since a barrier release records it still parked on its own frame while a later co-presence event records it on the rejoin frame), the map ghosts those nodes, and a line's green history begins at the rejoin frame. The "a live line wins the node it shares with a ghost" rule keeps working, with the single carve-out of that line's own pre-merge frames; another line visiting one of them still turns it green. The menu was lopsided in the same way and is fixed with it: a live-line rewind entry reaching back past a rejoin now reads `⏪⏪ rewind L0 here — out of the merge at ⟨F⟩`, the words the swallowed lines' entries always used — before that, three routes into one rejoin offered three different-looking buttons for one act, and the plainest of them was the one that would take the whole rejoin off the room.

**Absorbed routes are offered for every passage the map offers an undo for** — which is the merge entry's own `available`, read straight off `mergeRewinds`, and nothing else. They used to re-run `mergeRewindPlan` instead, which asks something NARROWER: a plan refuses a rejoin with anything built on top of it (`superseded`), while the entry beside it reads available because the undo walks that cascade off first — so a room that forked three ways and rejoined twice offered the undo for the older rejoin but no rewind entries on the route of the line it swallowed. The number an absorbed route's MENU names is kept honest by the check next to it: a snapshot whose number a non-retired line is holding today offers no route, because the undo would give that route a different one. (These routes were once also painted as grey dashed "ghosts"; that is gone — the map paints everything the room walked green, see [score-map.md](score-map.md).)

**A convergence undoes whole, in one gesture (owner).** Lines converging on one frame merge in **pairs** — three arrivals are two events, because `applyRecombine` runs once per arrival — but the operator watched **one** thing happen at that node and undoes that one thing: every line that met there comes back on the node it came from, in one click. `orch.mergeConvergenceEvents` names the passage and the runtime walks it **newest-first**, which is still the only order `mergeRewindPlan` allows: the later event's snapshot is the only record of where the population it swallowed came from, so the older one is **superseded** until the newer is undone and becomes available the moment it is (the supersession check over the append-only `mergeEvents[]`, whose array order IS chronological). The pairing therefore stays what it always was — the record of how the room got there — instead of a queue of clicks. Same convergence = **same survivor, same frame, same dive, and the survivor never left the rejoin point in between**. "Same dive" is the survivor snapshot's complete dive stack — every sub and its return landing (`orch.diveContextKey`, the key co-location already uses) — plus the main-flow trail each dive saved on entry (`mergeDiveIdentity`). The sub NAME was not enough: a sub history restarts on every entry, so two visits to one sub leave identical sub trails, and a survivor that rejoined on the same sub frame on two separate visits had both folded into one undo. The return landing separates visits that dive different ways; the saved trail separates visits that dive the same way (the later dive saved a longer main trail); arrivals within one dive share both and still group. The trail test: the older event's survivor trail is a prefix of the newer one's and every entry past the prefix is the rejoin frame itself. That one test reads both merge paths (a barrier release records the survivor still parked on its own frame, a co-presence merge records it standing on the rejoin frame, so the trail grows by exactly that frame between the two) and still tells a **loop** back to the same frame apart as the separate passage it is. The map lists the passage once, on its newest event, naming every line that comes back out; the folded older ones raise neither a second button nor a "undo the later merge first" note about a merge on this very frame. That note is still printed when the merge to undo first genuinely sits on **another** frame.

**The window.** `mergeEvents[]` is append-only in memory (`active` → `undone`/`expired`) and kept for the life of the session — except that an `undone` event is dropped when the state is written (`compactPersistedEvents`, as for splits, §8.3) and an `expired` one is written with only the fields its note and its trail corroboration read; an event stays `active` for the whole of it, and nothing the runtime does ends that any more (the room checkpoint rewind, which used to, now walks the rejoins it passes back instead — §8.1). An event is `expired` only when it **arrives** that way: a state file whose participants carry no `uid` for the room to corroborate. From then on — the undo is refused with an explanation rather than offered and got wrong (the map prints the note once per frame, only when nothing there is still undoable) — but the snapshots are NOT compacted away, so the routes it recorded stay on record even though the canvas no longer paints them. Ordering within the window is still strict: `mergeRewindPlan` is a single step and refuses while anything newer stands on the same population (`superseded`); the cascade above is what makes that a walk rather than a wall.

Undoing a merge also **lifts the `blockedByMerge` mark** it put on any split event it crossed (§8.3) — the populations are separable again — unless another merge crossed the same boundary and has not been undone. An **expired** merge keeps blocking: its own undo can never run, so the mixing it did is final. (Only a state file the loader could not corroborate produces one — the runtime expires nothing.)

**Every rewind can be refused, and says so.** A menu is built from a projection and the room keeps moving under it, so all rewinds can turn a click away — and each used to return a boolean nobody read: the operator answered a `confirm()` promising a topology change and got silence, indistinguishable from a bug. The refusal now travels back as `MSG_REWIND_REFUSED {kind, reason, frame, moved}`, the map states it in the operator's terms, and the lines snapshot is re-pushed with it (a refusal usually means the menu was stale). **All five entry points report**: the split undo, the merge undo and the per-line rewind did from the start, and the **room checkpoint rewind** (`no-checkpoints`, `not-a-checkpoint`, `line-unplaced`, `frame-missing`) and the **sub rollback** (`unknown-line`, `not-in-sub`, `stale-sub`, `stale-trail`, `stale-frame`, `frame-missing`) joined them — the room rewind's silence being the worst of the three, since it is the one operation that also rearranges the room's topology on the way (§8.1). Both validate their whole plan before touching a line, so their refusals are always clean no-ops. Three ordering rules keep a refusal honest: the **race guard runs before the walk** (`structuralGuardHolds` — the chain commits step by step, so leaving the guard to the final step let a stale click take a whole rejoin off the room and then collapse nothing); `splitRewindOptions` offers an entry on the strength of a cascade only for the refusals a walk-back actually repairs (`no-live-descendants`, `parent-unavailable`), never for one it cannot, and `mergeRewindOptions` applies the same discipline to the one refusal no cascade can lift — an event with fewer than two participants is `nothing-to-separate` up front rather than `superseded` now and refused after the walk; and **`moved` tells the truth about a part-way walk**. A cascade commits each step as it goes and nothing can put those back (each is a validated snapshot restore in its own right), so `splitRewind`/`mergeRewind`/`undoStructuralChain`/`mergeRewindConvergence` return `{ok, moved}` rather than a bare boolean and the map says "some of the steps it takes off first WERE undone" instead of "Nothing moved". The same rule closed a quieter hole: a convergence that came apart only halfway used to answer `true` — any progress counted as success — so the operator was told three lines had separated when two had.

**Identity is the uid, in both directions.** Numbers recycle, so neither answer a number gives can be trusted: a merge a line really walked through can name a number it no longer has — its own undo re-creates it on the lowest FREE one — and a merge it never saw can name the number it holds today. `mergesBehindLine` and `splitRewindPlan`'s parent lookup therefore key on `uid` (`childLineUids[0]`, then `parentLineUid`, number last), and the map applies the same test through `survivorLineUid` and the lines snapshot's `uid`. A state file written before uids existed is repaired on load (`migrateStructuralIdentities`): lines are stamped, split events are stamped only where the room corroborates the number — a child number must resolve to a line whose own `splitAncestors` names the event — and anything uncorroborated is marked `expired`, which already reads everywhere as "not offerable, and blocks any walk that would cross it". Guessing would be worse than expiring: a wrong uid drags an unrelated branch into a cascade. **The convergence grouping keys on it too**: `mergeConvergenceEvents` used to gather its siblings by the survivor's NUMBER and lean on `stayedAtRejoin` to tell two passages apart, which it cannot do when both survivor trails reduce to the rejoin frame and nothing else — two unrelated rejoins at one frame then read as a single convergence, and the walk committed the newer event before refusing the older one with `survivor-unavailable`, leaving the room half-separated. `sameSurvivorRoute` compares the survivor snapshots' uids, falling back to the number only for records written before uids existed. **`expireMergeEvents` was the last place still asking the number**: the room rewind handed it `lines.map(l => l.id)`, so a passage could be reached through a number an unrelated fork happened to be holding, and it was changed to match `lineUid` first. The function is gone entirely — the room rewind undoes the rejoins it passes rather than ending them (§8.1) — and its replacement, `mergesBehindRoomRewind`, is keyed on the uid from the start: it asks `mergesBehindLine` the same per-line question, and that keys on the route.

**The operator can see what an undo will do, and that it did it.** Three gaps closed together, all of them about the same thing: an undo is the largest gesture in the tool and it was the least legible. **Where the lines land** — behind the newest rejoin the ghosts draw the landings, but reaching an OLDER merge draws nothing at all, so `⏪⏪ undo merge here — also undoes 2 later steps` asked the operator to commit with no idea where three lines were about to appear. Every available merge entry now carries `landings` (`mergeRestoreLandings` in bin/www, walking the convergence newest-first through the same `preMergeLanding` the undo uses, so the OLDEST step wins the survivor exactly as the runtime gives it), and all three confirms that separate a convergence name them: `They land L0 → ⟨A2⟩, L1 → ⟨B2⟩, L2 → ⟨C2⟩.` **What is not restored with them**: a landing on a hold-until frame is flagged, because the undo restores the topology but **not the wait that produced it** — restored lines land un-parked and phase-less (above), so a barrier-release merge undone puts its lines back on the barrier frame with nothing holding them there, and the passage replays as a co-presence merge rather than the barrier release it was. That is the rule every rewind follows and it stays; the confirm just stops letting the operator discover it mid-piece. **That it happened**: a refusal already travelled back, while success said nothing at all — the operator answered a confirm promising a topology change and was left to infer from a redrawn canvas. `MSG_REWIND_DONE {kind, frame}` now answers every one of the five entry points, and the map shows back the sentence its own confirm promised (held since the click: after the undo the entry is gone, so the receipt could not be written afterwards). It is a timed panel on the canvas, not the status line, which every lines push overwrites — and a rewind sends several.

**The two halves of one gesture now agree.** `restoredLineIds` was built in merge order, so a convergence that merged in pairs put the last line to arrive in the middle: the button read `merged here as L0, L2, L1` and the confirm it raised read `They land L0 → ⟨A2⟩, L1 → ⟨B2⟩, L2 → ⟨C2⟩` — one act, two orders, and the unsorted half reads as a fault to whoever is looking at it mid-piece. Both sort by NUMBER now, as `mergeRestoreLandings` always did. The other half of that pairing is a caveat that only ever lived in the button's comment: **both lists name the numbers the REJOIN recorded**, not the numbers the lines come back on. An absorbed line's number goes into the pool at the rejoin, and undoing a passage frees the numbers its own cascade took — so the two agree in every ordinary case — but a fork on an unrelated population is not in that cascade, keeps the number it holds, and the route is re-created on the lowest free one (`freeLineId`). The landings quietly made the confirm name what the button was careful only to record; the FRAMES are exact either way, and it is the label that can shift.

**And the menu says one thing once.** Two shapes were lopsided in the way §8.4's earlier fixes were about. A node that a whole convergence lands back on carried one entry per line — `⏪⏪ rewind L0 here`, `⏪⏪ rewind L1 here`, `⏪⏪ rewind L2 here` — three buttons for three identical outcomes, since each of them undoes the same passage and then walks its line nowhere; `collapsedMergeLandingHtml` offers it once, as the act it is, and returns to the per-line entries the moment anything there is a real rewind of its own. And the live survivor's confirm ended `Only L0 then moves any further` about a line the undo alone had already placed — the ghosts' `atLanding` wording, which the survivor's entry never got. **A still-undoable split or rejoin is also marked on the canvas now**: a merge point stays reachable for the whole session, but once its passage stopped being the newest one nothing drew it — the ghosts come off and the node carries no marking — so the only way to reach an older rejoin was to remember which frame it was and click it. It carries a `⏪⏪` badge, and the legend explains that glyph in both places it appears (a badge, and the menu's mark for an entry that changes the room rather than one line).

**And an older passage is reachable from the nodes its lines come back to.** Ghosts were painted only for a passage nothing was standing on then (above — they now cover every offered passage), and a split truncates the trails it forks from (§6) — so the moment a rejoin stopped being the newest one, every frame its lines would land on went quiet: `A2` said "no line's current trail reaches here — no rewind" while the undo on `MERGE1` was one click away and would put `L0` on that very node. The rejoin's `⏪⏪` badge was then the only way in, which asks the operator to remember which frame the rejoin was on, mid-piece. The landings are already known — the server works them out with the same `preMergeLanding` the undo uses — so a landing node now carries the same one-click undo the rejoin node does (`remoteMergeLandingHtml`, reading the entry's own `landings`; the race guard sent is still the REJOIN frame). It is one act reachable from wherever the operator remembers it, which is the rule the rest of §8.4 already follows — and it is offered **only where the menu would otherwise be empty**, since anything the structural, ghost or per-line sections put on a node belongs to the newest passage and already offers the same undo through its own routes.

**One gesture drives the displays once.** A cascade is several validated steps — `undoStructuralChain` takes the later events off, and a convergence comes apart in pairs — and every step drove its own displays, so one click sent a device two or three `MSG_SHOW`s: the states the room was in the middle of discarding first, the real one last. On the score `FORK → MERGE1 → FORK2 → BARRIER → MERGE2`, undoing `MERGE1` sent each device `⟨BARRIER⟩`, then `⟨FORK2⟩`, then its real landing. Two things are wrong with that. Devices FLASH through frames the room is throwing away, with their sounds, which mid-piece reads as the tool losing its place. And the ORDER is not actually guaranteed: `ws-client`'s `onWsMessage` runs a message whose `t` has already passed inline and defers every other by `setTimeout`, so once the session's buffer duration is small enough for the computed delays to straddle zero — `preloadDuration` is an operator field with a minimum of 0 — a step's show lands AFTER the final one. Verified in the browser at buffer 0: two of three devices were left showing `⟨BARRIER⟩` while the server had them on `⟨B2⟩` and `⟨C2⟩`, and stayed there until their line next moved — a performer playing a page from the branch just discarded, and a `cid` the server will refuse their next tap on. The steps RECORD the lines they touched now (`holdLineDisplay`) and the outermost gesture drives them once from the room as it finally stands (`openDisplayHold` / `flushDisplayHold` / `driveLineDisplays` in bin/www, opened by `splitRewind` and `mergeRewind`, flushed in a `finally` so a walk that stops part-way still tells the devices where they are). Everything else a step sends still goes out as it happens — phase cancels, barrier releases, admin pushes — because those describe the room's state rather than what a device renders, and they are addressed to line ids only that step still knows. This is the rule `mergeRewindConvergence` already followed inside one convergence (above: "no device is told about a line the next step is about to move it off"), read across the whole walk; `sendRestoredLineDisplays` and `splitRewindOne`'s own send are gone into it, and the devices in SUB view are captured once when the gesture opens rather than per step, since a step's own `MSG_SUB_EXIT` is suppressed with everything else and the line a device ends on may have been in the main flow all along. **A per-line rewind is one gesture too.** §8.2's rewind can take several merges off before it moves its own line, so it drove the rewound line's devices to their merge landing and then to the real target — the same flash, the same hazard. It opens the hold as well now. What kept it out at first was its final send: a line rewound INSIDE its dive gets a plain `MSG_SHOW`, because the device is already in the sub and the index is sub-relative either way, and a hold that only knew "this device is in some sub" would have turned that into a `MSG_SUB_ENTER` re-injecting a sub it was standing in. So the hold records WHICH sub each device is rendering, not merely that it is: `driveLineDisplays` compares that against the view the line ends in and sends the mode message only when the mode actually changes. Verified in the browser — a rewind inside `Tetra1` sends one plain show and the device stays put; a split undo collapsing three mid-dive lines sends each of them one `MSG_SUB_EXIT` onto the fork.

**And the operator is told when a line comes back with nobody on it.** A merge undo hands a route back to whoever was walking it, and if that performer has since gone home the line comes back **dormant** — an outcome the confirm's "N lines go back, and every device goes back to the line it was on" does not prepare anyone for. It was there to be read on the canvas (`L2·0p`) and nowhere else. `MSG_REWIND_DONE` carries `emptied` now — the lines dormant after the gesture that were not dormant before it, matched on `uid` so a line the undo re-created on a recycled number does not read as one that was already empty — and the receipt finishes `L2 has nobody on it now — dormant until someone joins.` Deliberately a receipt and not a confirm: predicting it means simulating `spreadMergeLatecomers` through every step of a cascade, and a confirm that names the wrong line as about to be left alone is worse than one that says nothing. All five rewinds report it, since a room rewind can strand a line the same way.

**A landing entry stopped naming its own frame twice.** Reached from the node its line comes back to, the confirm opened `Bring L0 back out of the merge at "MERGE1" — onto "A2", the frame it came into it from?` and then went on to read `They land L0 → ⟨A2⟩, L1 → ⟨B2⟩, L2 → ⟨C2⟩` — A2 twice in one breath, which reads as the sentence having lost track of itself rather than as one line landing there. `mergeLandingSentence`'s `exceptLineId` already existed for the line that walks ON after the undo; both landing entries (`requestAbsorbedRewind` at its trail end, and `remoteMergeLandingHtml`'s `landsHere`) pass it now, so the list is `The others land L1 → ⟨B2⟩, L2 → ⟨C2⟩`. The one thing that does NOT come off with it is the barrier note: a line that STAYS on its landing is exactly the line that needs to hear the hold-until there is not re-armed, so `staysAtLanding` keeps it in that scan while dropping it from the list.

**Two numbers in the confirm stopped reading as faults.** The **latecomer count** is of devices the undo reassigns, and `mergeLatecomerCount` counts registered-but-absent ones because `rewindMergeStructure` really does sweep them — but the sentence said "the 4 devices that joined since", and an operator reads that against the room in front of them. Four while three are playing looks like the tool being wrong rather than like somebody having gone home, and doubting the count mid-piece means doubting the whole gesture. It reports `{total, live}` now and ships both (`latecomers`, `latecomersHere`) — on the detail bodies AND on every push's `structuralEvents` summaries, which the map copies onto the entries it holds (`syncLatecomerCounts`): the bodies are fetched once per topology and a join is not a topology change, so a map left open never mentioned the latecomers at all until it was reloaded; the clause appends `— 3 here now, 1 registered but away` only when they differ, so the ordinary case is unchanged. And the **landing list** is grouped by destination: a barrier release parks every line on one frame, so it came out `L0 → ⟨BARRIER⟩, L1 → ⟨BARRIER⟩, L2 → ⟨BARRIER⟩` — three clauses saying one thing, which reads as the sentence having gone wrong rather than as the lines really landing together. `L0, L1, L2 → ⟨BARRIER⟩` now, with the separator only doing more work once something is grouped so that one line per landing stays character-for-character what it was.

### 8.5 One gesture at a time, and what the operator agreed to

Every rewind is a **multi-step walk** over shared state — the cascade first, then a convergence in pairs, then the line itself — with `await`s in the middle, and `connection.on("message")` dispatches without waiting for the previous message to finish. Two of them interleaving corrupts the display hold (the second gesture records its lines into the first's, the first flushes them mid-move, and the second has nothing of its own left to flush — devices stranded on a discarded frame, which is the very thing the hold exists to prevent). All five rewinds therefore run under a **per-session gesture queue** (`runExclusively` in bin/www); the queue never rejects, so a failing gesture cannot wedge the room's rewinds. `MSG_BARRIER_RELEASED` — the map's force-release, force-advance and sub-eject, which is the same kind of thing told apart the same way by its payload — was off that queue and is on it now: `forceAdvanceStragglers` can reach `applyRecombine` and record a rejoin on the very population a rewind validated its chain against a moment earlier. The display hold itself is **per session** for the same reason (it was one module-level variable, so two ROOMS rewinding at once collided on it: the second found it taken, drove each step's displays immediately, and flashed its devices through the frames the walk was discarding).

A rewind is also a **promise**, and only half of it used to be pinned. `structuralGuardHolds` checks that the event is still on record, still active, still on the frame the menu named; it says nothing about the CASCADE — "this first undoes 2 later steps, the split at ⟨FORK2⟩ then the merge at ⟨MERGE2⟩, so everything the room played after them is discarded" — which is most of what the operator is agreeing to. The click therefore carries the cascade it was shown (`cascade`: `kind:eventId` steps for the two structural undos, bare event ids for the room and per-line ones) and the server re-derives it before anything commits; the rule is **containment**: a walk that would take off a step the operator was not shown is **`stale-cascade`**, refused as a clean no-op, while a walk that takes off FEWER is allowed (the room took that step off by itself, and nothing extra is discarded). Symmetric refusal was the first cut and was too brittle — a room rewind’s list comes from `roomCheckpointOptions`, which reads the live population, so one performer closing a tab between the push and the click turned a legitimate rewind into a refusal. A client that sends no `cascade` is trusted exactly as before.

The map's confirm is an **in-page panel**, not `confirm()`, for the same reason: a native dialog blocks the page script, so the map stops drawing the room it is describing and every push queues behind the answer — which is how a confirm promising two steps came to commit four. Live instead, the panel re-checks its own premise on every push and closes itself with a note rather than letting the operator commit to something the room has already moved past.

### 8.6 Recovery tools, in general

Force-release and rewind split cleanly: **force-release = skip the wait** (irreversible — per #11 a released barrier never re-arms); **rewind = replay and satisfy the wait properly** (re-opens closed paths, possibly taking the route to a missed target this time). "Unreachable" is never permanent while the rewind tool exists.

### 8.7 What the operator is told afterwards

`MSG_REWIND_DONE` goes to **every** operator tab, not only the one that clicked (a second map open on the same room otherwise watched its canvas rearrange with no account of why). The clicking tab shows the sentence its own confirm promised; the others compose a plainer one from `kind` + `frame`.

**Every rewind answers, including the one that fell over.** `dispatchRewind` reports a refusal or a receipt down every path it takes, and an exception was the one way past both — the ws dispatcher's own catch logged it server-side and the operator got silence, which is the failure mode these two messages exist to remove, in its worst form: a walk commits step by step, so a throw part-way through has very likely already moved the room. `handleSelectHistory` wraps the dispatch and answers `internal-error` with `moved: true`, which also clears the sentence the map has been holding since the click.

`emptied` names each line the gesture left with **nobody on it**, with the frame it is standing on. The frame is there because the two ends of the gesture do not name lines on the same basis: the confirm names the numbers the REJOIN recorded ("merged here as L0, L1, L2"), while this names the numbers the lines actually came back ON — `freeLineId` hands a route the lowest free number when an unrelated fork is holding its own — so the operator could read "L0, L1, L2 come back" and then "L4 has nobody on it". The frame keeps its word either way.

A **merge entry is offerable** when the event is separable (two or more participants) and either its own plan is available or the cascade will clear it. "The cascade will clear it" now includes the cascade's SPLIT steps: `undoStructuralChain` runs each through `splitRewindPlan`, which refuses a split a later merge mixed with another subtree, and a walk that hits one stops half-way with the room already changed. `chainSplitsCanUndo` asks the records that question before the operator commits — a block lifts only if every merge still holding the split is one this same walk undoes first — which is the rule `splitRewindOptions` has always applied to its own entries.

### 8.8 A split needs somebody to divide

Two rules, both learnt from one room that reached **16 lines with two performers in it**:

1. **A line with no population does not split.** A grouped window opens for *every* line in the track group, empty ones included, so an unpopulated line parked on a frame that is both `session-track-group` and `session-split` divided nobody into two empty lines on every release. Guarded at both call sites with `lineHasPopulation` (bin/www); such a line falls through and advances whole, like any other unpopulated grouped line.
2. **A branch nobody took is born `dormant`, not `active`** (`applySplit`). `planSplitPartition`'s `counts` are final by the end of `applySplit` — live members plus the #13 offline-registry sweep — so a zero means nobody at all, present or absent. Born `active` such a line was immortal by construction: dormancy is driven by a device *leaving* (#11) and a line born empty never had one to lose, so nothing demoted it, it held its number for the session, it was counted in "N lines (N active)", and `activeLines` kept handing it back to the grouped split.
   **…and a branch only ABSENT performers took gets the grace window** (`resolveSplit` → `startAttritionGrace`). The sweep counts an offline device, so that child is not a "nobody" branch and is born `active` — but nobody is connected to it, and nobody is left to leave, so it had the same immortality by a different road: active and empty for the rest of the session. Balancing then did the damage, because 0 players beats any populated line: in the owner's room the fork at `J` swept two closed tabs onto the `K` branch, the room finished on `END`, and the next two joiners were both put on `K`. Now the fork opens the #11 grace window on every active child with no population: a swept device that reconnects in time keeps its branch (`ensureLineAssignment` cancels the timer, as for any blip), otherwise the line goes dormant and joiners are placed by #13 — onto the room's `END`. Reproduced both ways in the sandbox on `-test- Session lines` (2 players on one branch, one closed before the fork at `J`, the other taking `M`): without it the joiner landed on `K`, with it on `END`.

Together these are what made the growth **exponential**: every line standing on the grouped split frame forked on every release, so the count doubled — 1 → 2 → 4 → 8 → 16, with 15 split events recorded at the same frame. The trigger in practice is a **room rewind to the checkpoint containing the split frame**: it lands every non-retired line back on it, and the next release forks all of them.

Dormant is what such a line actually is: no population, revivable by #11 for the next joiner — who then takes the road nobody took, which is the right landing for it — never waited for at a barrier, and never again offered to `activeLines`.

### 8.8a A split only clears the votes of the line it divided

Voting windows on independent lines overlap routinely — nothing synchronises them — and `resolveSplit` used to finish by clearing `currentVoteTo` on **every connection in the session**. A split resolving on `L0` therefore wiped the choices of everyone mid-window on `L2`. The cached tally hid it for a tick (`line._lastVoteCounts` stands in when the live tally goes empty), and then a later vote recalculated from the cleared connections and another split's partition read them directly — so performers on a line that had nothing to do with the fork were sent somewhere they did not choose.

The set to clear is the population whose window just closed, and it has to be captured **before** `applySplit` runs: that call rebinds each member to a child, so afterwards `line.id` no longer names the devices that voted. `lineConnections(…, line.id)` taken at the top of `resolveSplit` is that set, and nothing outside it is touched — not another line in the room, and not another room's line of the same number.

### 8.9 One release, one undo

A track group's release divides **every** populated line standing on a split frame (`resolveGroupVoting`), so one thing the operator watched happen is as many split events as there were lines. Unfolded, the map offered a button per event and each click undid a fraction of the release — the same defect merges had before `mergeConvergenceEvents`, one event kind over. The forks of one release now share a `gestureId` (stamped by `applySplit`, allocated per release), `orch.splitGestureEvents` reads them back newest-first, and `splitRewind` walks the whole set with each member's own cascade taken off first.

It does NOT fold the way a convergence does. A convergence happens on one frame, so the merge menu can hide its older halves; a release divides each line on **its own** frame of the group, and an operator looks at the node the fork happened on. So every member keeps its node and its button, and each button undoes the whole release — named in the label ("undo the release that forked L0 here … + 1 other fork from the same release") and in the confirm, which lists the siblings and where they are. Availability is **all of it or none of it**: a release with one member out of reach offers no button anywhere, because half a release undone is a room no single gesture ever produced.

A fork with no `gestureId` — a lone choice window closing, or any event recorded before this — stands alone, which is exactly what it was, and reads exactly as it always did.

### 8.10 A room rewind undoes the FORKS too

The rule §8.1 follows — a rewind to a checkpoint the room crossed as *n* lines must arrive with *n* lines — was only half implemented. The rejoins made since the checkpoint came off (`mergesBehindRoomRewind`); the **forks** did not. So the room could come back with MORE lines than it left with, and on a checkpoint frame that is itself a split frame the extra lines landed back on it and forked again on the next release. That is what turned rewind-and-replay into multiplication rather than restoration.

`splitsBehindRoomRewind` is the mirror, and it reads "since" from the only record that survives: a split TRUNCATES its children's trails (§6), so no child can say where the room was when it happened, but `parentHistory` is the parent's trail as the fork found it. If that trail had already reached this group, the parent was at or past the checkpoint when it divided and the fork comes off; a fork the parent made before ever reaching the group is older history and stays — which is what keeps lines that crossed the checkpoint *separately* from collapsing into one.

Forks and rejoins are walked as ONE list, newest-first by the session-wide `seq` (`compareStructural`, the ordering every chain walk uses), because a fork built on a rejoin has to come off before it. The whole list is validated before anything moves, and the plan is recomputed afterwards — the line set has changed completely. The confirm names the forks (`undoesSplits`, a warning line) exactly as it names the rejoins, and they ride the `stale-cascade` guard together.

**Resolved by this:** replaying a split no longer accumulates anything. A rewind to the checkpoint puts the room back to the lines that crossed it, and the replay mints one dormant husk for the branch nobody took — which the next rewind takes off again.

### 8.11 Smaller symmetries repaired in the same pass

Three places where a rule had been implemented for one event kind, or one code path, and not its mirror:

- **An EXPIRED fork is a note, not an empty menu.** An expired rejoin has always published `{available:false, reason:"expired"}` so its node can say why there is no undo; `splitRewindOptions` filtered expired events out entirely, so the fork's node rendered nothing at all — indistinguishable from a bug, which is the shape this menu has spent so long learning not to have. Nothing expires a fork at runtime; `migrateStructuralIdentities` does, on load, when a saved room's records cannot be tied back to its lines.
- **A cascade's MERGE steps are validated too.** `chainSplitsCanUndo` exists because a cascade's split steps carry refusals a walk cannot repair; `chainMergesCanUndo` is its mirror, and both menus now ask it (`unseparable-step`). It cannot fire on a record the runtime writes — `planRecombine` dedupes, so an event exists only when it absorbed somebody — which is an invariant of one function held in place by nothing, and a migrated or hand-edited state file is outside it.
- **The global hold toggle reaches the dormant lines.** `MSG_PAUSE` was widened to every non-retired line; `MSG_CHECK_HOLD` fifty lines away still addressed only the active ones. A dormant line therefore kept `isHolding` from before a global hold-OFF, with no timer to end it, and the first performer to revive it (#11) landed on a line whose taps `MSG_TAP` drops in silence — the stale-hold wedge a mid-hold state restore produces, reached without a restart.

### 8.12 Every undo button names its own frame

A node can carry more than one ⏪⏪ button: the rejoin that happened **there**, and — because an older passage stays reachable from the nodes its lines come back to — the undo of a **later** rejoin somewhere else. ⟨Z⟩ in the owner's own session was both, and the two read:

    ⏪⏪ undo merge here (1 → 2 lines, merged here as L0, L1) — also undoes 1 later step
    ⏪⏪ undo the merge at ⟨B-prime⟩ — L0 comes back here

The first one's confirm then said *"This first undoes 1 later step — the merge at ⟨B-prime⟩ —"*, so the operator looking for B-prime found the frame they wanted named in the confirm of the button that was **not** it, said yes, and undid both rejoins. The room came back with three lines where two were expected, and the engine had done exactly what it was asked.

Two changes, and neither is cosmetic:

- **Buttons name their frame, never "here"** — `undo the merge at ⟨Z⟩`, `undo the split of L0 at ⟨A⟩` — so two undos on one node are told apart by the only thing that distinguishes them.
- **A cascade is named, not counted**, in the button (`— also undoes the merge at ⟨B-prime⟩`, up to two then "and N more") and its sentence leads with the target (`To reach ⟨Z⟩ this first undoes …`). A count tells the operator there is something they cannot see yet; the whole question on a node with two undos is *which*.

### 8.13 A rejoin is not behind its own frame

The per-line rewind undoes the rejoins it reaches back past (§8.2), and where
"past" begins is read from the survivor's snapshot. Both shapes of rejoin were
measured the same way, and one of them is off by one:

- A **hold-until barrier release** snapshots the survivor still parked on its
  own frame; `runRejoin` advances it to the rejoin frame immediately after. The
  recorded index is strictly pre-merge — standing the merged line back on it
  would put the swallowed lines' devices on a frame their route never touched,
  which is the whole of §8.2 — so the rejoin *is* behind it.
- A **co-presence merge** finds the survivor already standing on the rejoin
  frame and moves nothing. That index is the MERGED line's own position, the
  frame every participant walked to. A rewind onto it asks nothing of the
  rejoin.

Read as one rule, the rejoin node offered both

    ⏪⏪ undo the merge at ⟨B-prime⟩ (1 → 2 lines, merged here as L0, L2)
    ⏪⏪ rewind L0 here — out of the merge at ⟨B-prime⟩

and the only way to put the line back on that frame was to take the rejoin off
— which the button beside it already did. The map was drawing that same frame
as the merged line's live green history while it said so: `survivorGhostRoute`
ghosts the route **into** a rejoin and never the rejoin frame itself, so the
two halves of the map had already disagreed.

`survivorRewindFloor` now answers the question per event — the recorded index
for a co-presence merge, one past it for a barrier release — and both the
server (`mergesBehindLine`, and through it `lineRewind`, the cascade race
guard and the room rewind) and the map compare against that. On the rejoin
node the second entry is a plain `⏪ rewind L0 here`; one frame further back it
is still `⏪⏪ … — out of the merge at ⟨MERGE1⟩`.

### 8.14 A rejoin outlives the identity that made it

A room rewind takes off the rejoins and the forks made since the checkpoint
(§8.10). It found the forks and missed the rejoins, and the two rules failed
together in the case where it matters most: the owner played the whole score
with four players and rewound to the track group they had crossed as **two**
lines, and the room came back as **one**, with all four on it.

The reason is that the two kinds of event are recorded against different
things. A fork is measured against the checkpoint in the PARENT's recorded
trail, which survives the fork. A rejoin is recorded against the identity that
**survived** it — and a fork mints new identities for its children. So on a
route that merged at `H`, merged again at `J`, then forked at `J`, every line
standing in the room afterwards was a child of that fork, and both rejoins,
recorded against the parent, answered "not mine" to all of them. The rewind
undid the fork and left the rejoins standing, so the lines they had swallowed
never came back.

`ancestorGroupOccurrence` already walks that ancestry — it is how a line whose
trail a fork truncated is placed on the checkpoint at all — so the walk now
also reports the **route** it took: each ancestor, as the identity its rejoins
were recorded against, paired with the index in that ancestor's trail the
rewind is returning to. `mergesBehindRoomRewind` asks the question once per
point: of the line as it stands today, and then of each line it used to be.

The index is the half that keeps this honest, and it is why the answer is not
simply "undo everything". Measured at that ancestor's own occurrence of the
group, the rejoin at `J` is behind the landing and comes off, while the rejoin
at `H` — the one that ran **as** the room arrived at the checkpoint, with the
survivor standing on `H` — is not (§8.13) and stays. The room crossed that
group already merged, so it comes back merged: three players on `H`, one on
`I`, which is where they were.

An intermediate ancestor — a fork whose parent never reached the group — is
pinned to index 0 instead: its whole trail lies after the checkpoint, so every
rejoin it survived comes off.

The preview is the same call, so the confirm names what the gesture does: "1
rejoin made since then is undone on the way, so L0, L2 stand on their own
frames again."

## 9. Sub-frame delivery

Main-score frames remain pre-inlined in the baked page (`server_state/{id}.rev-{revision}.html`; every baked file carries the build's revision, and readers resolve it through `session.bundleFile(kind)` so a rebuild switches page, content and graph together) ([database.js:428-477](../database.js#L428-L477)). Sub-score frames are built by `buildSubFramesContent()` (same id/`<a>` rewrite as the parent loop, resolved within the sub) into `${id}.rev-{revision}.subs.json` (gated; absent for vanilla) and **fetched on demand** via the read-only route `GET /session/:id/sub/:subName` → `{framesHtml, frameList, soundList}` (mirrors the streaming + apicache approach of [routes/session.js:15-37](../routes/session.js#L15-L37)). The client injects fetched sub-`<svg>` into `#SubSessionContent` (a constant child of `#MainContent`, shown as `display:contents` so sub frames adopt its layout), caches per sub, and swaps `window.listFiles` (restore via `window.parentListFiles`); `update-view` carries an explicit frame DOM id (`svg{idx}` vs `sub-{name}-{idx}`) so [audio-player.js:785](../public/javascripts/audio-player.js#L785) routes correctly. Sub-score sounds load via the existing on-demand Howler path from `/data/{score}/Subscores/{sub}/Sounds/` ([audio-player.js:17](../public/javascripts/audio-player.js#L17)).

**Neither end of a dive is timed by the frame the performer tapped.** A tap carries the `holding` of the frame its LINK names, read off the device's ACTIVE frame list ([voting.js](../public/javascripts/voting.js) `getFrameHoldingDur`) — and a dive is the one move where that frame is not where the line ends up. Diving, the link is a `session-sub-start` `<a>` whose `href` is the **return landing**, so the tap speaks for a main-flow frame the line will not stand on until it comes back out, while it lands now on the sub's `START`. Exiting, the tap named the **sub-end frame** — a marker no device ever displays, since reaching it pops the line in the same tick — while the line lands one hop later on the return landing. So each landing took its holding period from the other frame: a return landing marked `holding="20"` played whatever the sub end said, and one marked `holding="false"` landed **phase-less** — registered _done_ on arrival, hold gate open, a satisfied `hold-until` there releasing in the same tick and, while a release still auto-advanced (§7.3), its paired `rejoin-at` taking the line straight off the frame. That is the eject's defect (§10) reached by the score's own route, and the reason a fix aimed only at the operator valve did not settle it. Both ends now drop what the tap seeded (`clearLinePhase`) and start the LANDING frame's own hold from the graph (`startArrivalHoldForLanding` → `landingHoldSeconds`, sub-aware) before the arrival is recorded — §7.1 has the full census, and the graph is the authority for every landing now, not just these two — so the landing is `arrived`, not `done`, and the barrier gate holds. The standby gap goes with the cancelled phase: a dive landing's hold begins as the frame is shown, so its dwell is exactly the authored value.

**And the sub-end itself is passing through, not resting.** The same landing rule has a second half, in the rendezvous registry rather than the clock. `recordArrival` tells an `arrived` landing from a `done` one by whether a hold is still running there — and for a sub-end that used to be true by accident: the ordinary advance set `isHolding` on any non-zero session default, whatever the frame said, so the sub-end rode along on it. Once the gate started reading the FRAME (§7.1), a sub-end authored `holding="false"` — the honest value for a marker no device ever displays, and what the authoring guide tells authors to write — began completing **on arrival**, and `tryReleaseBarriers` fired while the line was still inside the sub: one frame short of the return landing the release expects to find it on, and before that landing had started its own hold, so a `hold-until` naming the sub-end opened on a line that had not arrived yet — and, while a release still auto-advanced (§7.3), its paired `rejoin-at` took the parked lines straight off the return frame. The skipped target again, reached from the registry instead of the clock. So the rule is stated now rather than inherited — `recordArrival` marks a sub-end `arrived` whatever its `holding` says, and the DEPARTURE two statements later completes it, by which time the line is standing on the return landing with that frame's own hold running.

## 10. Session manager controls across lines

- **Pause / global refresh:** session-global, apply to all lines (as today, [bin/www:658-680](../bin/www#L658-L680)).
- **Hold:** SM hold needs a scope decision (all lines vs a chosen line) — TBD with SM-UI work.
- **History / rewind:** the five map rewinds of §8; session-page dropdown read-only for session-lines rooms; vanilla keeps its per-step jump.
- **Force-release valve** (decision #12): admin force-release for a stuck barrier, incl. group waits (panel entries `group:<name>`).
- **SM visibility (implemented):** per-line device counts / frame occupancy ship two ways: the line-distribution panel on the session page (admin-only, `payload.lines` on `MSG_SHOW_NUMBER_CONNECTION`, refreshed on every line landing), and the standalone **live score map** (`/session/:id/map` — full score DAG with per-line badges, barrier/sub state, history trail and a click/tap rewind menu; works for vanilla scores as a single line). See [score-map.md](score-map.md).
- **Stuck-wait valves (implemented):** a waiting barrier offers the operator two opposite resolutions, in the map's `#session-map-barriers` strip, which is where a stall is actually watched developing. The session page carried an identical `#admin-barrier-panel` until it was removed (owner: the SM page had grown too much UI, and the map already had it) — the panel and every valve it wired up, none of which the map shares code with. The server side is unchanged: `notifyAdminsBarriers` still broadcasts the admin-flagged `MSG_BARRIER_WAITING` to all admin connections, which is what the map's strip consumes; the session page simply ignores the `data.admin` variant now. A performer's own parked/straggler banner is a different message and is untouched, as is the `#line-distribution` panel. **release** / **release all** let the barrier go without the lines it is waiting for (the original decision #12 valve). **force advance** / **advance all stragglers** do the reverse: each straggler teleports onto the barrier — a track group's onto its least-occupied frame (the same landing rule a dormant-line revival uses, decision #13 revision, so forced arrivals fill empty track slots instead of doubling covered ones), a hold-until's onto the target it is still missing — and the barrier then releases down the ordinary path. Advancing is `confirm`-gated (it moves performers' devices) while releasing is not. A forced arrival is still an arrival: if the group is _still_ waiting on someone else, the line just brought in parks with the rest. The map also badges a straggler line `⏩` against a parked line's `⏳`. Note a force-release is the one release with no landing behind it, so it explicitly re-pushes the per-line snapshot — without that the panel and badges kept showing the wait the operator had just resolved. `finalizeAttrition` needed the same push for the same reason: the ws close handler pushes immediately, but dormancy lands 8 s later on a timer, so the panel kept a line listed as active and still-waited-on well after both stopped being true.
- **A hold-until waits on lines too.** Until then only a track group had stragglers; a stuck hold-until listed `[parked: …]` and nothing else, and the operator's only valve was the release — waive the target. Its stragglers are now the lines that can still REACH a target nobody has reached (`holdUntilStragglerTargets` in bin/www over the pure `orch.holdUntilStragglerTargets`), which is the deadlock guard's own "the wait is still viable" set read the other way round: a line that guard counts as keeping the wait alive is exactly a line the operator can end it with. They get the same "your move" banner and `⏩` badge, and advancing one sends it to the target, so the wait ends **met** rather than waived. Two carve-outs: a line already standing ON the target is listed but not banner-prompted — the barrier is waiting on its holding period, not its travel (the same rule that keeps a blocked _occupant_ off a group's straggler list), and its advance ends that hold instead of moving it; a line for which the score offers no landing at all is still listed, with its button inert and saying why (that is the case release exists for).
- **A sub-score target is a landing too.** A `Score/Frame.svg` ref names a frame inside a sub, and the advance drops the line onto it: `subAdvanceLanding` resolves the sub, the frame's index in its list, and — for a line not already inside — the authored dive it should count as having taken, preferring a `session-sub-start` link on a main frame the line can still reach, so its return landing after the sub is the one the score would have given it. A line already in that sub keeps its dive (its sub history and return landing survive the move); a line in a _different_ sub leaves that one first, as a main-flow advance does. It lands on the **target frame, not the sub's START**, for the same reason the main-flow advance does: the barrier waits on that frame specifically, and a branching sub might never route the line through it. Execution is `advanceLineIntoSub` (`clearLinePhase` → `enterSub` → `setCurrIdxTo` → `recordArrival`), the sub twin of `teleportLineForAdvance`. Devices are told in the right vocabulary: **`MSG_SUB_ENTER`** for a line that is now inside a sub (idempotent — the client caches the injection), `MSG_SUB_EXIT` for one that has just left one, and a plain preloaded `MSG_SHOW` for main-to-main; the two sub messages go out even while paused, with the pause placeholder restored right after.
- **"Advance this line"** is the same command narrowed to one line: `MSG_BARRIER_RELEASED {frame, advance: true, lineIds: [id]}`. It is offered on the **map's node menu**, on the node the straggler is standing on — which is where the operator is already looking when a group or a hold-until has stalled the room on one line — and as a `⏩ <line>` button per straggler in both barrier strips, replacing the plain `[waiting on: L2,L3]` list that named the problem without offering the fix. The entry names its destination up front (`⏩ advance L3 to ⟨G⟩`, or the group's name where the landing is only picked at run time). The barrier-wide **force advance** deliberately sends no `lineIds`: it should cover whoever is a straggler when the server runs it, not whoever was one when the snapshot was pushed — while the per-line ids ARE filtered against the freshly computed set, so a stale one is ignored rather than moving the wrong line.
- **Ejecting a line out of a sub-score** is the same move with no barrier behind it. A dive is a session of its own: the authored sub-end frame was the only way out of one, and a line stopped short of it — voting stalled, a performer gone, a branch that never routes there — could be freed only by the room-wide checkpoint rewind, which discards every OTHER line's progress to rescue one. `ejectLineFromSub` pops it forward onto the landing the score itself would have given it (`subStack[0].returnHref`, the OUTERMOST dive's return frame, so a nested dive goes with it), reusing `teleportLineForAdvance` whole: clear the phase, pop the stack, land, re-check the barriers, `MSG_SUB_EXIT` to its devices. It is deliberately **not** straggler-gated — a line stuck in a sub stalls the room whether or not a barrier happens to be waiting on it — and it rides `MSG_BARRIER_RELEASED {eject: true, lineId, sub}`, told apart by payload shape the way `MSG_SELECT_HISTORY` already carries five rewinds. `sub` is the race guard (refused if the line has since popped out on its own), and a dive whose `returnHref` no longer resolves against the live frame list is refused too — the map prints the reason instead of offering a dead button (`lines[].subReturn` carries the resolved landing, or null). **An abandoned dive completes nothing:** the sub frame the line walked away from is not marked done (`_lastReachedRef` is cleared first), so a hold-until waiting on a target inside that sub keeps waiting — the release is the valve for that one. **This is also the rewind-out-of-a-sub that §8.2 excludes**, composed rather than built: `exitSub` restores the main-flow history `enterSub` saved, so the moment the line is out, its pre-dive trail is back in the map's menu and the ordinary per-line rewind walks it back to where it dived from.
- **Rolling a line back out of its dive** is the eject's opposite, and the sub-crossing rewind §8.2 had ruled out. The destination is **any main-flow frame the line has already visited**: `mainHistoryStateForLine` is its main trail exactly as the dive left it (`savedHistories[0]` while inside a sub), and `selectedIdx` picks an entry of that trail. Omitted, it defaults to the trail's own current entry — the frame carrying the `session-sub-start` link the line followed — so **"undo this dive" is this command's default case, not a second command**. Everything resolves off the saved trail before a field is mutated, so a refused race guard leaves the line exactly where it stood. Landing is `lineRewind`'s: `historyIndex` then `setCurrIdxTo`, which drops the redo entries after the chosen step — rolling back to the fork itself splices nothing and skips the duplicate push, so undoing a dive leaves the trail precisely as the dive found it. The two valves land by **opposite rules**, and this is the distinction to keep straight: an **eject** is ordinary travel, so it lands through `afterLineArrived` and JOINS whatever the score put on the return frame; a **rollback** is a rewind, so it lands on the fork and deliberately does **not** park there — an operator rewind is authoritative, exactly as `lineRewind`, `jumpScoreForSession` and `roomRewind` are. It keeps `lineRewind`'s registry rules too (registry NOT restarted, `latestGroupArrival` untouched — the ROOM did not rewind; the sub frames the line reached stay reached, because it really did stand on them — §7.4 preserves that rule exactly, `visitedSubFrames` being the one trail nothing trims, while the main frames it rolls back off stop being corroborated like any other). The line lands **un-dived** — dives are navigation-triggered, so a reposition never re-dives on its own — and its next window may take the sub again or take another link, which is what makes this the "let them try that passage again" tool. Rides `MSG_SELECT_HISTORY {lineId, exitSub: true, sub}` (the rewind channel, as the eject rides the barrier channel), with `sub` as the same race guard; `lines[].subOrigin` carries the destination for the menu, null when the score no longer has that frame.
- **An operator advance clears the line's phase** (`clearLinePhase`, shared with the room rewind). The old path cancelled the holding/voting **timers** but left `isHolding` set, and a line that lands still "holding" with no timer to end it never marks its landing frame done (`recordArrival` gates on `!isHolding`) — a hold-until waiting on that frame, or a track group syncing on its occupants' holds, would then wait for an edge that could never come. It also clears the stale per-device `currentVoteTo`, so the line's next window starts from a clean tally.
- **…but a landing that JOINS the score again starts a fresh one** (`startArrivalHoldForLanding`). The phase clear above is right for an advance — it exists to END a wait — and wrong for the **eject**, which is ordinary travel: landing phase-less, its return frame was marked _done_ on the spot and `holdPendingAtFrame` (the gate that stops a satisfied barrier releasing in the same tick as an arrival, §5/#6) read false, so a hold-until whose targets were already met released instantly and — with a single paired `rejoin-at`, back when a release still advanced (§7.3) — took the line off the frame **14 ms** after its device had been told to show it. The landing starts the frame's own holding period before `afterLineArrived`, taking the duration from the frame's authored `holding` value (`landingHoldSeconds` reads the graph the parser built) and skipping it entirely on `holding="false"`. **Both ends of a dive land the same way**, and for the same reason — no tap speaks for where they put the line (§9). The barrier advances deliberately keep the old behaviour: a fresh hold there would be the opposite of the command, exactly as `endHoldForAdvance` cuts a straggler's hold short rather than extending it.
- **Quiet-device marker (implemented):** a device whose page is gone but whose socket is not — a tab frozen into the browser's back/forward cache, a wedged renderer — keeps answering the ws protocol keepalive from its **network stack**, so transport liveness cannot see it; the only tell left is that its 60 s `MSG_PING` stopped. Every connection therefore carries `lastSeen` (stamped on accept and on every app-level message), `updateNumberOfConnectionForSession` sends each line a `quietSince` (the oldest `lastSeen` among its performers), and past 3 min the operator panel appends `(quiet Nm)` — highlighted like a straggler, because a quiet device on a waiting line is exactly what a wedged group looks like — while the map badges that line 💤. The panel re-renders itself every 15 s: a stalled room sends no pushes, which is precisely when the age is being read. **Reported, never acted on** — a backgrounded phone throttles its ping too, and dropping a live performer would change line population and group waits. Transport liveness is separate: the ws server pings after 20 s of silence and drops after a 10 s grace, now spelled out on the `WebSocketServer` rather than inherited from the library's defaults, and reaps a genuinely dead peer in ~30 s.
- **The panel's `stragglers[]` is computed before the payload is built**, not after: `notifyAdminsBarriers` opens by calling `refreshStragglerNotices`. Refreshing afterwards left the panel exactly one edge behind, which is invisible in steady state and obvious after a restart — devices reconnect one at a time, each reconnect changes who is being waited on, and the panel would name only whoever reconnected first while the line panel (a different payload) named everyone. For the same reason a **player** reconnect re-pushes the panel; the admin-only push further down fires only for admins.

## 11. Client & display changes (verified against client code)

The client renders whatever frame/voting state the server addresses to it, so **most client code is line-agnostic** — the bulk of the work is server-side per-line addressing, plus these targeted client changes:

- **Per-line show needs no client change.** `parseMessage` ([session.js:56](../public/javascripts/session.js#L56)) handles `MSG_SHOW`→`showImageAtIndex` ([session.js:312](../public/javascripts/session.js#L312)), which shows `#MainContent svg[id="svgN"]` and hides the rest. The client just shows whatever index its line is told.
- **Stay/winning glow needs NO client change.** The glow follows the server's `winningVoteId` ([voting.js:111](../public/javascripts/voting.js#L111); [voting.js:201](../public/javascripts/voting.js#L201) already handles `winningVoteId==="stay"`). The whole stay rule is implemented server-side by setting each line's `winningVoteId`.
- **Split frames replace that one glow with a per-device destination marker.** A `session-split` frame has no winner to glow, so the server sends no `winningVoteId` there and hands each device `splitDestinationVoteId` — the child link it is currently going to. The client marks it with the same look the winner has (`winning`, plus a `destination` class as the DOM/QA hook), which is what a **play-mode** device sees: it cannot tap, so this marker is its only cue about where it is being sent. The marker is shown for the **whole** window, in both modes — including after the performer taps. A first cut dropped it once it equalled the device's own vote (on the reasoning that `current-vote` already marked that link); in testing that read as the app losing track of the device the instant it was guided, so the two now **stack** on the one link — `vote-indicator current-vote destination winning` — saying both "my choice" and "where I land".
- **Chosen and assigned destinations are told apart by colour.** A destination the device did **not** choose was placed by the server's balancing, so it renders **blue** with an **"auto"** label in the middle of the circle; a path the performer tapped keeps the orange of an ordinary vote. Colour is the _only_ difference, and it is enforced structurally: `.vote-indicator` declares `--marker-ink` (border + text) and `--marker-glow` (pulse/gradient, as raw RGB channels so stops can vary the alpha), every shared rule and both keyframe sets read those variables, and `.vote-indicator.auto` re-points them and nothing else. A first cut instead duplicated the play-mode rule with a blue gradient — and the `background` **shorthand** in it silently reset the `background-repeat`/`position`/`size` that the orange rule sets in the three declarations right after its own shorthand, so the blue glow tiled from the top-left instead of pulsing from the centre. Duplicating the rules is what made that possible; sharing them makes it unrepresentable. Every play-mode device is in the auto case (it cannot tap), as is a guide-mode device that has not chosen yet. The label is always in the DOM and its legibility is left to CSS — play mode's `color: transparent` hides it — because switching modes never re-renders these indicators, so a JS mode test would go stale the moment the performer toggled guide mode. When the assigned link also carries votes, the count and the label stack inside the circle (`.vote-count` + `.auto-label`, `flex-direction: column`). Because the destination is an assignment, not a tally, it is marked even on a link with zero votes: an indicator with no count is injected for it and survives the "remove everything not in the dic" sweep.
- **Indicator positioning was broken and is fixed (pre-existing, all scores).** `injectVoteIndicator` assigned bare numbers to `style.top`/`style.left`; a CSS length without a unit is an invalid declaration, so the CSSOM dropped it and every injected indicator fell back to the stylesheet's `top/left: 0` — piling every vote bubble and the winning glow into the viewport's top-left corner instead of over its link. Now `` `${…}px` `` (verified headless: the indicator's centre lands exactly on the link's centre). This affects vanilla scores too; it is not session-lines-specific.
- **The destination moves during the window, on purpose.** Non-choosers are balanced across the children, so one performer's tap can re-balance another device's slot; the marker follows the live partition exactly as the winning glow follows the live tally. It is pushed the moment the window opens — **with the tap that opened the window already counted** (`startVotingForSessionWithDelay`'s `initiator`, which survives the reset of the previous window's votes), so the tapper is never told it was auto-assigned somewhere its own echo is about to overwrite; everyone else is a straggler, spread evenly, so nobody stares at an unmarked frame — and again from `resolveSplit` off `applySplit`'s **own** assignment, so the last marker a device sees before the frame changes is always the child it actually got.
- **Device identity.** `ensureDeviceId()` on session load (§6); `did` added to every `sendToServer` envelope ([ws-client.js:148](../public/javascripts/ws-client.js#L148)). The reconnect path already re-pings ([ws-client.js:44](../public/javascripts/ws-client.js#L44), [ws-client.js:70](../public/javascripts/ws-client.js#L70)), and `sessionStorage` persists across refresh/eviction restore, so membership restores automatically. **Leaving the page now closes the socket** — `pagehide` → `cancelReconnectTimer` + `teardownSocket`, `pageshow` with `event.persisted` → reconnect, registered beside the existing `visibilitychange` wiring in both entry points ([session.js](../public/javascripts/session.js), [session-map.js](../public/javascripts/session-map.js)). A tab that navigates away is not necessarily gone: the browser can freeze the leaving document into its back/forward cache with the WebSocket still OPEN, and the server, which counts live connections, went on counting it as a device. An operator round-tripping between the session page and `/sm` gained one phantom player per trip (the session page carries no link back to `/sm`, so the way back is the **Back button** — exactly the navigation that bfcaches the page), and a phantom is counted by `performerLineConnections`, so it also held a line's population up while never tapping.
- **The opening handshake retries until it is answered.** The three-ping calibration is what makes a page live: its last reply runs `viewDidLoad()`, which starts the 60 s `pingTimer` and asks for the display. Nothing else drives it, so an unanswered **opening** ping was terminal — the socket stayed OPEN while the page went deaf, its taps went nowhere, and it never retried, because the retry timer is one of the things the calibration was supposed to start. Worse, the server tags a connection (`sessionId`, `lineId`, `isStaff`) **inside** its `MSG_PING` branch, so an unanswered handshake also meant the device was counted **nowhere**: its line read empty, and the group / hold-until waits that should have been waiting for that performer dissolved instead. `startHandshake` ([ws-client.js](../public/javascripts/ws-client.js)) now re-sends `MSG_PING` every 2 s while the handshake makes **no progress**, stopping on `isReady`, on a non-OPEN socket, and from `teardownSocket`. Any reply is progress — each one decrements `pingCountToReady` — so a healthy calibration, which completes in milliseconds, never sends a second ping. Both entry points share `ws-client.js`, so the map page is covered by the same change.
- **A device is told its line when it JOINS**, not only when one changes under it. `MSG_LINE_ASSIGNED` was sent on a split, a rejoin, a structural undo and a rider re-home — every case where a device _moved_ — so a device that simply joined or reconnected carried `window.lineId === null` for the rest of the session while the operator panel had it placed correctly. Nothing functional reads the global, but it is the first thing anyone reaches for when a live room misbehaves, and a null there sends them hunting a binding bug that does not exist (a reconnect after a server restart showed the whole room as line-less). The `MSG_NEED_DISPLAY` handler now sends it beside the display, right after `ensureLineAssignment` has bound the connection — riders included, since a spectator is bound to the line it watches and its re-home already reports the same way. Session-lines rooms only; a vanilla room's traffic is unchanged.
- **Split tap** reuses `handleSelectLink`→`MSG_TAP` ([voting.js:33](../public/javascripts/voting.js#L33)); the server interprets taps as line choices during the split window (optional "choose your path" labeling). Client handles `MSG_LINE_ASSIGNED` (stores its own line).
- **Barrier banner:** `#barrier-waiting-indicator` + body class, driven by `MSG_BARRIER_WAITING`/`MSG_BARRIER_RELEASED` (also reused by group-arrival parking). It carries **two roles**, stamped as `data-role` and sharing the one clear signal: `parked` → "waiting for other lines…" for a line held at a hold-until or group-arrival barrier, and `straggler` → "Your move, proceed when ready…" for a line a waiting group is held open **for**, which until then was told nothing at all and so never knew the room was standing still on its account. The straggler prompt is styled in the app's accent (it asks for an action) while the parked banner keeps the passive grey it shares with the loading indicator. **Both are guide-mode only** (owner): they are navigation messages, and in play mode they merely sat over the frame a performer was sounding. Visibility is a stylesheet rule on `.guide-mode … [data-role]`, never an inline `display` — switching modes does not come back through the JS, so a mode test there would go stale the instant a performer guided their device.
- **Who is a straggler** is the group's `incomingIds` minus the lines that are themselves parked (pure `orch.groupStragglerIds`), plus every line that can still reach a waiting hold-until's missing target (pure `orch.holdUntilStragglerTargets`). A parked line stays in `incomingIds` — the group really is waiting on it, and dropping it would release the group early — but it cannot proceed, so "your move" would be a lie and its own banner already explains the wait. Blocked _occupants_ (`blockedIds`) are never stragglers: they have arrived; the group waits on their hold, not their travel — and for the same reason a line standing on a hold-until's target is listed for the operator but not prompted. The flag rides on `line.stragglerFor`, which is transient (absent from BMLine's persistence allowlist), holds the barrier's own ref (`group:<name>` or a hold-until frame name) and is re-derived from live positions on every barrier edge. A group wait wins the flag when a line is the straggler of both: it is the wait the line can do least about.
- **One display transition at a time, and the newest one wins.** Two things made a display message unsafe to apply on arrival. It can be **stale**: the server addresses frames to a moment in the near future (`t`, the preload window) and the page holds them in a timer, so a rewind, a dive or a reconnect in between leaves the held message describing a display that has already been replaced. Every display-driving message therefore carries `dr`, a revision that only goes up, allocated in `sendToLine`/`sendDisplayToClient` so no call site can forget it; `displayRevOk` drops anything older than the last applied. The revisions come from **one server-wide sequence**, not one per line: devices change lines (a split's fresh child, a merge's survivor, a structural undo's restored line), and per-line counters made a device that had applied revision 20 on L0 reject its new L1's revisions 1…19 as stale. Ordering alone does not retire what the OLD line had already queued for the device, so `MSG_LINE_ASSIGNED` and `MSG_BEGIN_SPLIT` are revisioned too, and a page whose line CHANGES takes the assignment's revision as its floor (`assignLine`); a device told the id it already has (a merge survivor) keeps its floor and its own line's queued frame. `MSG_PAUSE` is revisioned as well — it paints the placeholder, and an unrevisioned pause let the `MSG_SHOW` an advance had queued for the preload deadline land after it and put the frame back up in a paused room. A socket generation could not do this job — a rewind and the stale `MSG_SHOW` it overtakes live on the **same** socket. A fresh socket resets the epoch (`resetDisplayEpoch`), so a restarted server counting from zero is believed. And it can be **superseded while it is being applied**: entering a sub-score fetches and injects that score's frames, and the room does not stop while that is in flight. `renderDisplay` takes a generation number before starting and re-checks it once the assets are ready, so a slow dive can no longer pull a performer back into a sub the server has already left; in-flight fetches are deduplicated per sub-score, and a failed one leaves a legible, tappable recovery state (`#sub-load-indicator`) that re-asks for the display rather than a console line nobody sees.
- **A reconnect gets one authoritative snapshot, not a handful of optional messages.** `MSG_NEED_DISPLAY` used to be answered with several independent messages, each sent only when its state was ON — so everything OFF was said by nothing at all and a returning page kept whatever it was left holding. A device that dropped inside a sub and came back after the line had returned got a bare `MSG_SHOW`, which changes the index without restoring the main frame list; a device that missed a barrier release came back with the banner still up; a PAUSED room sent no context-bearing message at all, so a page loaded mid-pause had no idea its line was mid-dive until the resume handed it a sub-relative index. Session-lines rooms now answer with a single `MSG_SHOW` carrying `snapshot: true` — `sub`, `showIdx`, `isPause`, `isHold`, `holding`, `voting`, `waiting`, every field present including the nulls — applied in one step by `applyDisplayState`. It rides on `MSG_SHOW` rather than a new constant so the shared built HTML (vanilla included) stays byte-identical. The `/map` tab is excluded: it renders no frames and a sub-relative index means nothing against the main flow, so it keeps the `MSG_SUB_ENTER` it has always ignored. Vanilla rooms keep today's messages exactly.
- **Sub-frames.** Fetch + inject per §9; audio `registerSubFrames` + `getSoundLink(name, ctx)`.
- **SM live controls** (hold/pause/history/global-refresh) live in the **session page** admin UI ([session.js:28-41](../public/javascripts/session.js#L28-L41)), not `routes/sm.js`.

## 12. Server message protocol

- `sendToLine(line, time, payload)` (+ `…WithDelay/…Admins` variants; filter connections by `conn.lineId`); keep `sendToAllClients`/`sendToAllAdmins` for session-global messages (`PAUSE/GLOBAL_REFRESH/CHECK_HOLD/SHOW_NUMBER_CONNECTION/FINISH`).
- `MSG_PING` / `MSG_NEED_DISPLAY` carry `deviceId`; server resolves `lineId` via `ensureLineAssignment` and replies with that line's current frame/voting/holding state ([bin/www:585-629](../bin/www#L585-L629)).
- **Every message below the handshake is checked before it reaches a line.** `MSG_PING` is what tags a socket with its room, its staff standing and its line, so before the handshake all three are **undefined** — and the rider rule below reads `isStaff === false`, which undefined is not. A socket that opened and sent `MSG_TAP` first was therefore neither refused as a rider nor admitted as a performer but simply unchecked: it fell through to `lineForConn`, which resolves an unbound connection to the room's first active line, and moved it. Knowing a session id and a frame index was the whole of the credential. Stated positively now, in `routing.isBoundToSession`: a message acts on a room only from a socket this server has already bound to **that** room, so a socket cannot reach room B by putting B's id in a payload either.
- `MSG_TAP` is line-scoped, plus the split-choice path. **A rider's messages stop at the door** (#12): after the `MSG_PING` branch has tagged the connection, anything from a connection that is not staff, other than `MSG_NEED_DISPLAY`, is dropped. A rider's page only ever sends those two, so the guard costs a legitimate spectator nothing and takes the rule off the client's honour system — a hand-crafted rider can no longer tap, vote, pause, toggle hold, rewind or work an operator valve.
- **A tap is a performer's gesture, and its destination is the score's to authorize** (`routing.canPerform`, `orch.validateTapTarget`). A `MSG_TAP` is accepted only from a connection bound to this room, authenticated as staff at the handshake **and** carrying valid credentials on this message, that is not an observation tool — a `/map` tab authenticates with the admin password and so passed every check there was, but it renders no frames and offers no links. It then acts on the line the connection is **bound** to, never on `lineForConn`'s fallback. The `selectedId` is resolved against the frame the line is standing on: the vote id's source frame must name that frame (the client index guards an index, and a line inside a sub indexes the sub's list, so an index alone does not say which frame), and its destination must be one the frame's own links offer. **The tap also carries the dive context it was made in** (`ctx`, `orch.diveContextKey`: every level's sub and return landing, `""` on the main flow), and on a session-lines score it must be the line's own. A filename and an index do not identify a frame across a dive — a main score and its sub can both open on `START.svg` at index 0 — so a main-flow tap delivered late, after the line dove, passed every other check and advanced the sub along a link the performer never saw. The server stamps `ctx` on every `MSG_SHOW`/`MSG_SUB_ENTER`/`MSG_SUB_EXIT`/`MSG_PAUSE` and the snapshot; the page records it only when a frame of that context is actually on screen (`window.displayContext`), so a tap on the old frame while a sub is still loading reports the old context and is refused. Three things went through that door before, because the instant-advance branch passed `Number(head)` straight to the playhead — a destination the frame does not link to (the tally path validates a winner; the instant path had no tally), a destination out of range, and `NaN`, which a real client produces by tapping STAY on a zero-duration frame. `setCurrIdxTo` refuses an out-of-range index as a last boundary, so no caller can leave a line resting on a frame no score has. A frame's window length comes from the graph too (`landingVotingDur`), on the same argument as `landingHoldSeconds` in §7.1: the tap reports the `voting` of whatever frame the page that sent it was showing.
- `MSG_SHOW_NUMBER_CONNECTION` carries `playerCount`/`riderCount` (performers only — map tabs count nowhere) and, for session-lines rooms, `splitRewinds[]` + `mergeRewinds[]` + `absorbedLines[]` + `lines[]`, each line entry carrying its population, frame, sub/waiting/straggler/voting/holding state, trail/checkpoint, and `quietSince` (§10). `lines[]` is sorted by line NUMBER — `session.lines` is in creation order and a structural undo re-creates lines in the order it walks, so undoing a convergence left the operator panel and the map reading "L0, L2, L1"; sorted in the payload rather than in `session.lines`, whose own order is the runtime's business.
- `MSG_UPDATE_VOTING` is line-scoped (`sendVotingUpdateToLine`), **except on a split frame**, where it is per connection: `countDic` carries no `winningVoteId` and each payload gains `splitDestinationVoteId` — the vote id (`target#frame#slot`, built by the pure `orch.splitDestinationVoteIds`, so it is the id that link's `<a>` actually carries) of the child that device is going to, or `null` for a connection outside the partition. Sent to performers only; the map page ignores the message. Vanilla and non-split payloads are unchanged — the field is simply absent.
- New constants ([constants.js](../constants.js), wired through the jade `fn({...})` in [database.js:439-454](../database.js#L439-L454) and the `window.MSG_*` block in [session.jade:41](../views/session.jade#L41)): `MSG_LINE_ASSIGNED=16, MSG_BEGIN_SPLIT=17, MSG_BARRIER_WAITING=18, MSG_BARRIER_RELEASED=19, MSG_SUB_ENTER=20, MSG_SUB_EXIT=21, MSG_REWIND_REFUSED=22, MSG_REWIND_DONE=23`.
- `MSG_SELECT_HISTORY` payload variants: `{selectedIdx}` (vanilla jump; refused for session-lines rooms), `{group}` (room checkpoint rewind), `{lineId, selectedIdx, frame}` (targeted line rewind), `{splitEventId, frame}` (structural split undo).
- Every one of those answers back: `MSG_REWIND_REFUSED {kind, reason, frame, moved}` when it did not happen (§8.4), and `MSG_REWIND_DONE {kind, frame, emptied?}` when it did. The success message is deliberately thin — the map is holding the sentence its own confirm promised, and showing THAT back is what closes the loop; a fuller payload would only duplicate a projection the undo has just invalidated. The one thing it does carry is `emptied`, the lines the gesture left with NOBODY on them, present only when there are any: that is the half of the outcome the held sentence cannot know, because it depends on who was still connected when the undo ran.
- `MSG_BARRIER_WAITING` payload variants: `{frame, targets}` / `{frame, group}` (this line is parked), `{frame, group|barrier, straggler: true}` (a barrier is waiting for this line — `group` for a track group, `barrier` for the hold-until frame), and the admin-flagged `{admin: true, barriers[]}` — each barrier carrying `frame` (a hold-until frame name, or `group:<name>`), `targets`, `parked[]`, `stragglers[]` and, for hold-until entries, `advanceTargets` (`lineId → the frame a forced advance would land it on` — a `Sub/Frame.svg` value lands inside that sub-score; a straggler with nowhere to land at all is simply absent from the map).
- `MSG_BARRIER_RELEASED` is dual-use: outbound it clears whichever banner a line is showing; **inbound from an admin** it is the operator's valve — `{frame}` resolves one barrier, no frame resolves every waiting one, `{advance: true}` picks the opposite resolution (bring the stragglers in rather than let the barrier go without them), and `{advance: true, lineIds}` narrows that to named lines ("advance this line").

## 13. Persistence & restart

`saveSessionStateToFile` serializes via a versioned `toJSON()` **allowlist** (v1 flat → v2 `lines[]`; `migrateState` upgrades old files): `lines[]` (timers nulled, `isVoting` false; `BMLine.fromJSON` rehydrates — persisted fields include `isBarrierWaiting`, `pendingHoldUntil`, `subStack`, `savedHistories`, `visitedSubFrames`, `splitAncestors`, `status`), `deviceRegistry`, `reachedTargets`/`reachedGeneration`/`reachedGranted`, `latestGroupArrival`, `splitEvents[]`/`mergeEvents[]` (without their `undone` entries, §8.3) — dropping re-derivable `listFiles*`/`graph` ([database.js:519-536](../database.js#L519-L536), [database.js:629-648](../database.js#L629-L648)). On restart, rebuild lines and graph; **barrier state survives**: a lazy `rehydrateBarriers(session)` rebuilds the transient `_barrier` map from persisted line state on the session's first message after a restart, then schedules a deferred `tryReleaseBarriers` after the reconnect grace window (an immediate check would see zero reconnected devices and force-release); group waits are re-checked on rehydration too. Reconnecting devices restore their line via `deviceId`. A join that adds or moves a `deviceRegistry` entry saves the state there and then — it used to ride on the next unrelated write, so a device that joined just before a restart was a stranger to the room that came back.

**Writing the file is durable.** Saves are queued per session and published atomically (temp file + rename), because `fs.writeFile` truncates at open and writes afterwards: two overlapping saves used to interleave, leaving the longer payload's tail past the end of the shorter one's JSON and a state file the next boot could not parse. Boot is fault-tolerant to match — `loadStoredSessionStates` reads `${id}.json` only (never `.subs.json`, a rendered `.html`/`.svg`, or a `.json.tmp` leftover), and a file that still fails to parse is renamed to `.corrupt-<ts>` and skipped with a `console.error` instead of taking every other session down with it ([database.js](../database.js), [test/state-file.test.js](../test/state-file.test.js)).

## 14. Implementation architecture (as built)

Delivered incrementally on `feat/session-lines-v2` (chunks A–N: test harness/baseline → parser/graph/validator → graph wiring → `BMLine`+shim+versioned persistence → per-line messaging/device identity → per-line voting/holding → orchestrator → split → hold-until/rejoin → sub-sessions → track groups → SM controls → cleanup), under these standing principles:

1. **Gate everything** on `session.hasSessionLines`. A score with no `session-*` markup stays **byte-for-byte identical** in built output and behavior (proven continuously by a baseline snapshot diff of a vanilla score's `server_state` output).
2. **Pure logic in `lib/session-lines/`** — `parse.js` (frame-attr parser), `graph.js` (`buildGraph`), `validate.js`, `routing.js` (`sessionConnections`/`lineConnections`/`performerLineConnections`/`smallestLineId`), `orchestrator.js` (`planSplitPartition`, `holdUntilSatisfied`, `groupArrivalState`, `holdUntilReachabilityState`, `planRecombine`, track-group window helpers, `subReturnIndex`, `createOrchestrator(transport)`) — no express/ws requires, unit-testable without booting servers.
3. **Single-line invariant:** until a split happens, all new code reduces to today's behavior (`lines[0]`); a prototype accessor shim let `bin/www` be converted incrementally.
4. **Test harness:** dependency-free Node scripts in `test/` (`node:assert`, `test/run.js`, `npm test`; Node 14+), plus offline tools `bin/build-score.js <folder>` and `bin/validate-session-lines.js`.

**Critical files:** [database.js](../database.js) (BMSession/BMLine, graph build, versioned persistence, sub-frame build), [bin/www](../bin/www) (per-line voting/holding/messaging, split/barrier/rejoin/sub/group orchestration, device identity, rewinds), `lib/session-lines/*`, [routes/session.js](../routes/session.js) (sub-frame + map/graph routes), [routes/sm.js](../routes/sm.js) (graph-build + validation hooks on create/update), [constants.js](../constants.js), client: [views/session.jade](../views/session.jade) (`#SubSessionContent`, MSG wiring), [public/javascripts/session.js](../public/javascripts/session.js), [ws-client.js](../public/javascripts/ws-client.js), [voting.js](../public/javascripts/voting.js), [audio-player.js](../public/javascripts/audio-player.js); score map: see [score-map.md](score-map.md).

- **SM hold scope** across lines (all vs chosen line) — with SM-UI work.
- **Multi-exit sub-scores** (several `sub-end` frames → different main landings) — extension to the decoupled href mapping.
- **Session-page rewind UX:** the map's checkpoint / targeted-line rewinds could later also be offered from the session-page dropdown (modal design pending).
- **L5 (from the code review, Appendix A — accepted approximation):** group resolution edge cases — grouped lines that were _not_ voting when the group resolves get forced into standby + a stay-glow `MSG_UPDATE_VOTING`, and a line arriving at a grouped frame mid-window opens its own later window, so a group can resolve twice. Acceptable approximation of "begin/end together"; revisit if it bites.

- **Forcing a line out of a sub-score needs `MSG_SUB_EXIT`, not `MSG_SHOW`**. Only the _natural_ sub end (`exitSubSession`) sends it, yet **four** paths pop `line.subStack` server-side and then push a bare `MSG_SHOW` carrying a **main-flow** index — this entry said "three", and that miscount is exactly why the fourth survived two regression passes over the whole spec. The client's sole exit from sub view is the `MSG_SUB_EXIT` handler ([session.js:291](../public/javascripts/session.js#L291)), so those devices keep the sub's injected frame list and `frameContext`: `showImageAtIndex` looks for `sub-<name>-<idx>`, finds nothing, the display goes blank on a stale frame and the audio player throws `Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'noteIds')` on the unresolvable id. `forceAdvanceStragglers` sends it (and, while paused, re-sends `MSG_PAUSE` so the stale sub context cannot outlive the move and break the eventual unpause). `roomRewind` sends it too (fixed, same shape: capture `wasInSub` **before** unwinding `subStack`, then `MSG_SUB_EXIT` — plus a re-sent `MSG_PAUSE` while paused — instead of the main-flow `MSG_SHOW`, which the non-dived lines still get). Verified in a browser by A/B on a fixture whose track group sits _before_ the subs: with the fix every line returns to `frameContext.type === "main"` on its group frame; with it disabled the main-flow index resolved against the **sub's** injected list, so each device sat on an unrelated sub frame (`domId` still `sub-<name>-<idx>`) while the server believed the line was on the group frame — a silent desync, and the renderer wedged hard enough that a CDP screenshot timed out. **`splitRewind` was the fourth, found and fixed.** A structural split undo whose descendants are mid-dive rebinds every one of their devices to the restored parent and then addressed a single main-flow `MSG_SHOW` **to the whole line** — so on `-test- Session lines`, undoing the 3-way split at `A` while two lines sat in `Tetra2`/`Cube1` left those two devices rendering `sub-Tetra2-0` / `sub-Cube1-0` (`End`, an unrelated sub frame) while the operator panel read `L0: 3p @ A.svg`: the same silent desync, reproduced in a browser. Fixed the same way as the others but addressed per **connection**, because the rebind erases the distinction the other paths still have — the mid-dive connections are captured _before_ `rewindSplitStructure` runs and get `MSG_SUB_EXIT` (plus a re-sent `MSG_PAUSE` while paused) while the rest of the collapsed line still gets the `MSG_SHOW`. Map views are deliberately excluded from that set: they never render sub view. `fastForwardRevivedLine` has it too but is unreachable in practice: a revival is triggered by a device _joining_ a 0-device line, so its page is freshly loaded and never in sub view.

The jade copy is dead content — a device that never receives a barrier message simply carries it hidden — but it reads like the source of truth. Left alone deliberately: session.jade is the vanilla template, so editing it moves the byte-identical baseline.

**Known issues:**

- **State saved mid-hold restores `isHolding=true` with no timer** → taps refused until the SM toggles hold (pre-existing). The coordination half of this is closed: hold-until barriers and track-group hold sync read the frame's period and the clock, so such a line no longer wedges a rendezvous (§7.5) — it is the TAP gate that still reads the flag.
- **"Inactive line wedges group":** a group wait can be held open by a line whose device is gone but whose connection was never closed. One confirmed source of such zombies is now closed at the client: a tab frozen into the back/forward cache kept its socket — and therefore its line population — alive indefinitely (§11), while never tapping. Whether that was _the_ cause here is unproven; the symptom has not been reproduced since. The original theory in this entry is **wrong** and worth not re-deriving: the ws server does have a liveness timeout (ping after 20 s of silence, drop after a 10 s grace — measured reaping a non-responding peer in 30.1 s). It simply cannot see a socket whose network stack answers pings while its page is dead, which is now at least _visible_ as `(quiet Nm)` / 💤 (§10). **Not the reconnect wedge, either** (checked, and worth not re-deriving): a device whose opening handshake goes unanswered is dropped _before_ the branch that sets `conn.sessionId`, and every helper in [routing.js](../lib/session-lines/routing.js) filters on it — so such a connection cannot hold a barrier open, it is invisible to one. That is the opposite failure mode, and it is fixed at the client (§11). This item stays open.
- **The server still drops messages during its own boot window.** `BMDatabase`'s constructor calls `init()` without awaiting it ([database.js](../database.js)) while the ws server starts listening immediately ([bin/www](../bin/www)), so `messageHandle`'s `if (!session) return` silently discards anything that arrives before `loadStoredSessionStates()` finishes. It is a race and **boot duration is the dial** — the more saved sessions on disk, the wider the window (measured: 1 state file ⇒ every device reconnected cleanly, 42 ⇒ none did). The client-side handshake retry (§11) makes it survivable, since a device now re-pings its way in, but the drop itself remains: a `MSG_NEED_DISPLAY` or a tap sent inside that window is still discarded. The root-cause fix — await `init()` before the ws server accepts — was deliberately not taken, because it reorders boot for the HTTP server too, and that path already carries the apicache-404 scar on `/graph` (§ score-map).
- **Boot parses orphan `.subs.json` files as session state** (`loadStoredSessionStates` takes every non-`.html`/`.svg` file); harmless today (garbage `BMSession` early-returns on missing folder), worth a filename filter eventually.
- **Dev-only `wsPath` hack:** local working trees sometimes carry a hardcoded LAN ws URL in [database.js](../database.js) for phone testing — never commit it (`develop` uses `wss://${serverIp}`).

- **A merge became undoable (owner request):** a rejoin was the room's one irreversible structural step. The split had its undo, a line had its own trail, the room had its checkpoints — but once lines merged, the populations were gone: the survivor kept a single trail, so the only thing any rewind could offer was to walk that one line backwards with everybody still on it. A merge now records what it swallows (`mergeEvents[]`, persisted beside `splitEvents[]`): each participant's frame, trail, track group, dive and device ids, taken before a single field is touched. `MSG_SELECT_HISTORY {mergeEventId, frame}` puts exactly that back — every line on the node it came into the merge from, every device on the line it was on — and the map offers it on the rejoin node (§8.4). **Owner-tested on `-test- Session lines 2` (V/W → Z) and corrected in the same pass:** restoring the snapshot verbatim is right for a barrier release, which merges lines still parked on their own frames, but a co-presence merge catches every line standing ON the rejoin frame — so the undo put V's and W's lines back on Z, separated but not moved. `preMergeLanding` now steps such a line back one entry of its own trail (past consecutive re-visits, truncating the trail as any rewind does), which makes both paths land a line on the frame it came from; a line mid-dive, or one with nothing behind it, keeps its snapshot position. Two rules make it safe rather than approximate. Chained merges undo **newest-first** — a convergence of three lines is two events, and the older one is superseded until the newer is undone, then offered again, so a whole convergence walks back one step at a time (found in the browser pass: three lines arriving on `J` merged in pairs, and the first cut simply expired the earlier event, losing L1's separation for good). Everything else that rearranges the population **expires** the event — the merged line splits, a room rewind or a split undo repositions it — because from then on no snapshot describes it; the map says so instead of offering it. And because an `active` event keeps the absorbed line objects (and their numbers) alive, that same expiry is what returns `L<n>` to the pool — it runs _before_ `applySplit` allocates, so §6's recycling reading (`L0/L1` again at the next fork) is unchanged. Undoing a merge also lifts the `blockedByMerge` mark it put on any split it crossed, which is the first time that block has ever been liftable. **And the rewind the operator reaches for first now implies it** (owner — "why don't we undo merge automatically?"): the survivor keeps its pre-merge trail, so the map offers ordinary per-line rewind entries from before the rejoin, and taking one used to walk the whole merged line back — devices from the other route standing on a frame that route never touched. `lineRewind` now undoes every merge behind its target first (`mergesBehindLine`, newest-first), lands each restored line on the node it came from, and then continues on the clicked line alone; the confirm names the merges and the lines coming back out. The split needed no such rule: its children's trails start fresh at their own frames, so no per-line rewind can reach through one.
- **A convergence is one thing, so it undoes in one click (owner):** "once I clicked rewind L0, all previous lines [were still] merged into L0 — we should rewind all lines, each back to its respective previous node". Merging in **pairs** is an implementation fact — `applyRecombine` runs once per arrival, so three lines meeting on `MERGE1` a second apart is two events — and the first cut let that fact reach the operator: the map offered the newest event only ("undo merge here (1 → 2 lines: L0, L2)"), one click separated one line, and the older event then appeared as a second button to click again. The explicit undo now walks the whole passage (`orch.mergeConvergenceEvents` → `mergeRewind`, still newest-first, each step an ordinary fully-validated undo), and the map folds the passage into its newest entry so the button names every line that comes back out. A passage is bounded by the survivor never leaving the rejoin point (prefix-of-trail + only the rejoin frame past the prefix), which reads a barrier release and a straggler's later co-presence merge on the same frame as ONE meeting while keeping a loop back to it separate. Verified in a real browser on `-test- Merge rewind` (`run-session`): staggered arrivals on `MERGE1` → one click puts `L0/L1/L2` back on `A2/B2/C2` with their own devices; the `BARRIER` shape (`D`+`E` released and merged, `F` walking on afterwards, two events on `MERGE2`) → one click puts all three back on `BARRIER`; and the per-line rewind behind the same convergence still lands each line on its own node.
- **A merge point is a place the room can go back to all evening (owner):** "what I want is able to rewind back to multiple merge point later still possible, not losing the route path". The undo window was one structural step wide: `applySplit` expired the merge that produced the line it was forking, so on the very score the feature was tested against — `FORK → MERGE1 → FORK2 → MERGE2` — walking past `FORK2` put `MERGE1` permanently out of reach, ghosts and route and all. The expiry existed for a number, not for correctness: an `active` event held the absorbed line OBJECTS alive, and with them their numbers, so keeping merges undoable would have made the room's numbering climb against the recycling decision. Both halves are now solved separately. **The route is never thrown away**: snapshots are kept for the session (expiry no longer compacts them), so the map can always say where a line walked. **The undo cascades**: `structuralRewindChain` walks the splits and merges the room built on top of the one being reached for — transitively, over the same population — and `undoStructuralChain` undoes them newest-first, each an ordinary validated snapshot restore, before the target. **And the numbers are freed at the rejoin instead**: `reservedMergeLineIds` is gone, and a merge undo RE-CREATES the lines it restores (`freeLineId` → the number it had, or the lowest free one if a fork has taken it), so §6's `L0/L1/L2` reading is untouched. Two supports were needed under that. Every line now carries a durable `uid` (persisted, and restored by both undos), and every structural event records the participants' uids beside their numbers — without it a recycled `L2` would drag an unrelated branch into a cascade. And both event kinds carry one session-wide `seq`, since their ids come from separate counters and a cascade has to walk them in a single order. A room checkpoint rewind is the one thing that still expires a merge. **Devices that joined after the merge** are no longer dumped on the survivor: "the device that join later will be distributed balanced between lines once undo merge at that point" — performers are spread across the lines coming back (live and offline alike, riders excepted as spectators), the same balancing a split gives its unassigned members. Verified in a real browser on `-test- Merge rewind` (`run-session`): after `MERGE1 → FORK2 → BARRIER → MERGE2`, the `MERGE1` node still offered "undo merge here (1 → 3 lines: L0, L1, L2) — also undoes 2 later steps", and one click walked the `MERGE2` convergence and the `FORK2` split off and put `L0/L1/L2` back on `A2/B2/C2` with a device each; three latecomers on the merged line came out 2/2/2 instead of 4/1/1; the ghost entry on `B2` still brought `L1` back with the line object gone; and a per-line rewind of the survivor to `A1` still undid the merge on the way (§8.2 unchanged).

After a rejoin the map drew one route, the survivor's, so the only per-line rewind on offer walked the whole merged population back down a route two thirds of it had never taken; the frames where L1 and L2 actually played offered nothing. Absorbed lines are retired — they must stay out of every count, panel and `lines[]` entry — but while their merge is undoable their objects and trails are already kept for that undo, so they now ride a separate `absorbedLines[]` array carrying the trail the undo would RESTORE (`preMergeLanding`-corrected, so every entry is a real destination).
